Module 1: What is AI?

Definition

Artificial Intelligence (AI) refers to computer systems that can perform tasks that typically require human intelligence. These include understanding language, recognizing patterns, making decisions, and learning from experience.

Types of AI

  • Narrow AI (Weak AI): Designed for specific tasks — chatbots, recommendation systems, image recognition. All current AI is narrow AI.
  • General AI (Strong AI): Hypothetical AI with human-like general intelligence. Does not exist yet.
  • Superintelligent AI: AI surpassing human intelligence across all domains. Still theoretical.

Generative AI

Generative AI creates new content — text, images, audio, code — based on patterns learned from training data. Examples include GPT-4 (text), DALL-E (images), and Midjourney (art).

Large Language Models (LLMs)

LLMs are AI systems trained on vast text corpora. They predict the next word in a sequence, enabling them to generate coherent text, answer questions, translate languages, and write code.

AI Limitations

  • Hallucination: AI can generate plausible-sounding but false information
  • No real understanding: AI predicts patterns; it doesn't truly "understand"
  • Training data cutoff: Knowledge is limited to training date
  • Bias: Reflects biases present in training data
  • No common sense: Can fail at tasks requiring real-world reasoning

Common AI Myths

  • ❌ Myth: AI is conscious or has feelings → ✅ Truth: AI is a statistical pattern matcher
  • ❌ Myth: AI will replace all jobs → ✅ Truth: AI augments work; new roles emerge
  • ❌ Myth: AI is always correct → ✅ Truth: Always verify AI outputs
  • ❌ Myth: AI learns in real-time from users → ✅ Truth: Most AI is static between updates

Module 2: How AI Thinks

The AI process follows a simple pipeline:

🧠 AI Prediction Pipeline

Question/Prompt → Tokenisation → Model Processing → Probability Prediction → Response Generation

Key Concepts

  • Tokenisation: Text is broken into tokens (words or subwords)
  • Attention mechanism: AI focuses on relevant parts of input
  • Transformer architecture: The neural network design powering modern LLMs
  • Temperature: Controls randomness — lower = more focused, higher = more creative

Module 3: Good vs Bad Prompts

The Prompt Formula

📝 Perfect Prompt Structure

Role + Goal + Context + Format + Constraints

Examples

❌ Bad Prompt ✅ Good Prompt
"Write an email" "Write a professional apology email to a client about a delayed shipment. Tone: empathetic but professional. Length: 150 words. Include: apology, explanation, compensation offer, next steps."
"Explain photosynthesis" "Explain photosynthesis for a GCSE Biology student. Include: light-dependent reactions, Calvin cycle, and where each occurs in the chloroplast. Use bullet points."
