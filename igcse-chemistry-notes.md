# IGCSE Chemistry Notes (0620)

## 1. STATES OF MATTER

### Three States

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|----------|-------|--------|-----|
| **Shape** | Fixed | Takes container shape | Takes container shape |
| **Volume** | Fixed | Fixed | Fills container |
| **Particles** | Close, regular pattern | Close, random | Far apart, random |
| **Movement** | Vibrate in fixed positions | Move past each other | Fast, free movement |
| **Forces** | Strong | Moderate | Negligible |

### Changes of State
```
Melting: Solid → Liquid
Freezing: Liquid → Solid
Boiling/Evaporation: Liquid → Gas
Condensation: Gas → Liquid
Sublimation: Solid → Gas (and reverse)
```

**Heating curve:** Temperature stays constant during change of state (latent heat)

### Diffusion
- Movement of particles from high to low concentration
- Faster in gases than liquids
- Evidence of particle movement

### Brownian Motion
- Random movement of particles suspended in fluid
- Evidence for kinetic particle theory

## 2. ATOMIC STRUCTURE

### Subatomic Particles

| Particle | Charge | Mass | Location |
|----------|--------|------|----------|
| **Proton** | +1 | 1 | Nucleus |
| **Neutron** | 0 | 1 | Nucleus |
| **Electron** | -1 | 1/1840 | Electron shells |

### Atomic Number & Mass Number
- **Atomic number (Z):** Number of protons (determines element)
- **Mass number (A):** Protons + neutrons
- **Isotopes:** Same atomic number, different mass number (different neutrons)

### Electronic Configuration
- Electrons occupy shells around nucleus
- First shell: max 2 electrons
- Other shells: max 8 electrons (at IGCSE level)

**Examples:**
- Na (11): 2,8,1
- Cl (17): 2,8,7
- Ar (18): 2,8,8

### Ions
- Formed when atoms gain or lose electrons
- Metals lose electrons → positive ions (cations)
- Non-metals gain electrons → negative ions (anions)

## 3. THE PERIODIC TABLE

### Structure
- **Periods:** Horizontal rows (same number of electron shells)
- **Groups:** Vertical columns (same number of outer electrons)

### Group Trends

| Group | Name | Valence Electrons | Character |
|-------|------|------------------|-----------|
| **1** | Alkali metals | 1 | Reactive, soft, low density |
| **2** | Alkaline earth metals | 2 | Reactive, harder than Group 1 |
| **7** | Halogens | 7 | Reactive non-metals, colored |
| **8/0** | Noble gases | 8 (or 2) | Unreactive, monatomic |

### Group 1 — Alkali Metals
- Reactivity increases down group
- React with water: Metal hydroxide + hydrogen
- Stored in oil (react with air/water)
- Low melting points

**Reaction with water:**
```
2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂
```

### Group 7 — Halogens
- Reactivity decreases down group
- Displacement reactions: More reactive displaces less reactive
- Colours: F₂ (pale yellow gas), Cl₂ (green gas), Br₂ (red-brown liquid), I₂ (grey solid/purple gas)

**Displacement:**
```
Cl₂ + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br₂
```

### Group 0 — Noble Gases
- Full outer shell = stable
- Uses: Helium (balloons), Neon (lights), Argon (light bulbs)

### Transition Metals
- Properties: High melting points, strong, dense, coloured compounds
- Uses: Catalysts, alloys, electrical wiring
- Variable oxidation states

## 4. CHEMICAL BONDING

### Ionic Bonding
- Metal + non-metal
- Transfer of electrons
- Strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions
- High melting points, soluble in water, conduct when molten/dissolved

**Example: NaCl**
- Na → Na⁺ + e⁻
- Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻
- Na⁺ and Cl⁻ attract

### Covalent Bonding
- Non-metal + non-metal
- Sharing of electron pairs
- Can be single, double, or triple bonds

**Examples:**
- H₂: H-H (single)
- O₂: O=O (double)
- N₂: N≡N (triple)
- H₂O: Two O-H single bonds
- CO₂: Two C=O double bonds
- CH₄: Four C-H single bonds (tetrahedral)

### Metallic Bonding
- Metal atoms
- Lattice of positive ions in "sea" of delocalized electrons
- Properties: Conduct electricity, malleable, ductile, high melting points

### Giant Structures

| Structure | Bonding | Examples | Properties |
|-----------|---------|----------|------------|
| **Giant ionic** | Ionic | NaCl, MgO | High MP, soluble, conduct when molten |
| **Giant covalent** | Covalent | Diamond, graphite, SiO₂ | Very high MP, insoluble, variable conductivity |
| **Metallic** | Metallic | Cu, Fe, Al | Variable MP, conduct, malleable |
| **Simple molecular** | Covalent (intermolecular forces) | CO₂, H₂O, O₂ | Low MP, often gas/liquid, insoluble |

### Diamond vs Graphite
- **Diamond:** Each C bonded to 4 others, tetrahedral, very hard, insulator
- **Graphite:** Each C bonded to 3 others, layers, soft, slippery, conductor (delocalized electrons)

## 5. STOICHIOMETRY

### Formulas

**Empirical formula:** Simplest whole number ratio
**Molecular formula:** Actual number of atoms

**Calculating empirical formula:**
1. Convert mass or % to moles
2. Divide by smallest number
3. Round to whole numbers

### Moles
```
Moles = Mass (g) / Molar Mass (g/mol)
Moles = Concentration (mol/dm³) × Volume (dm³)
```

**Molar volume of gas at RTP:** 24 dm³/mol
```
Moles = Volume (dm³) / 24
```

### Reacting Masses
1. Write balanced equation
2. Calculate moles of known substance
3. Use mole ratio to find moles of unknown
4. Convert to mass

**Limiting reactant:** Reactant that gets used up first

### Concentration
```
Concentration (mol/dm³) = Moles / Volume (dm³)
Concentration (g/dm³) = Mass / Volume (dm³)
```

### Percentage Yield
```
% Yield = (Actual yield / Theoretical yield) × 100
```

### Percentage Purity
```
% Purity = (Mass of pure substance / Mass of impure sample) × 100
```

## 6. CHEMICAL ENERGETICS

### Exothermic & Endothermic

| | Exothermic | Endothermic |
|---|------------|-------------|
| **Heat** | Given out | Taken in |
| **ΔH** | Negative | Positive |
| **Energy level** | Products lower than reactants | Products higher than reactants |
| **Examples** | Combustion, neutralization | Photosynthesis, thermal decomposition |
| **Bonds** | Forming > Breaking | Breaking > Forming |

### Bond Energies
```
ΔH = Σ(Bonds broken) - Σ(Bonds formed)
```
- Breaking bonds: Endothermic (+)
- Forming bonds: Exothermic (-)

## 7. RATES OF REACTION

### Factors Affecting Rate
1. **Concentration** — More particles = more collisions
2. **Temperature** — Particles move faster, more energy
3. **Pressure (gases)** — Particles closer together
4. **Surface area** — More exposed particles
5. **Catalyst** — Lowers activation energy

### Collision Theory
- Particles must collide
- With sufficient energy (≥ activation energy)
- With correct orientation

### Measuring Rate
- Gas volume over time
- Mass loss over time
- Colour change over time
- Time for precipitate to obscure mark

### Catalysts
- Speed up reaction
- Not used up
- Specific to reaction
- Lower activation energy
- Provide alternative pathway

**Enzymes:** Biological catalysts (see Biology notes)

## 8. EQUILIBRIA

### Reversible Reactions
```
Reactants ⇌ Products
```

### Dynamic Equilibrium
- Forward and backward reactions occur at same rate
- Concentrations remain constant (not necessarily equal)
- Only in closed system

### Le Chatelier's Principle
> If conditions changed, system shifts to oppose change.

**Concentration:** Increase reactant → shifts to products
**Temperature:** Increase temperature → shifts to endothermic direction
**Pressure:** Increase pressure → shifts to side with fewer gas moles
**Catalyst:** Speeds up both directions equally — no shift

### Haber Process
```
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃   ΔH = -92 kJ/mol
```
- Conditions: 450°C, 200 atm, iron catalyst
- Compromise: Lower temp favours yield but too slow; higher pressure favours yield but expensive

### Contact Process
```
2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃
SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄
```
- Conditions: 450°C, 1-2 atm, V₂O₅ catalyst

## 9. ACIDS, BASES & SALTS

### pH Scale
- pH < 7: Acid
- pH = 7: Neutral
- pH > 7: Alkali

### Strong vs Weak
- **Strong acid:** Completely ionizes (HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃)
- **Weak acid:** Partially ionizes (ethanoic acid, citric acid)
- **Strong base:** Completely ionizes (NaOH, KOH)
- **Weak base:** Partially ionizes (NH₃)

### Neutralization
```
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O
```

### Salt Preparation Methods

| Method | Example | When to use |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Titration** | NaCl from NaOH + HCl | Soluble salt + soluble base |
| **Excess solid** | CuSO₄ from CuO + H₂SO₄ | Insoluble base/oxide + acid |
| **Precipitation** | PbSO₄ from Pb(NO₃)₂ + Na₂SO₄ | Insoluble salt |
| **Metal + acid** | ZnCl₂ from Zn + HCl | Reactive metal + acid |

### Tests for Ions

**Cations (flame test):**
- Li⁺: Crimson
- Na⁺: Yellow
- K⁺: Lilac
- Ca²⁺: Brick red
- Cu²⁺: Blue-green

**Anions:**
- CO₃²⁻: Add acid → effervescence, CO₂ turns limewater milky
- Cl⁻: Add AgNO₃ → white precipitate (soluble in NH₃)
- Br⁻: Add AgNO₃ → cream precipitate (partially soluble)
- I⁻: Add AgNO₃ → yellow precipitate (insoluble)
- SO₄²⁻: Add BaCl₂ → white precipitate

**Ammonia test:**
- Add NaOH, warm → NH₃ gas (turns damp red litmus blue)

## 10. REDOX REACTIONS

### Definitions
- **Oxidation:** Gain of oxygen / loss of hydrogen / loss of electrons
- **Reduction:** Loss of oxygen / gain of hydrogen / gain of electrons
- **OIL RIG:** Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons), Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)

### Oxidation States
- Element: 0
- Monatomic ion: Charge
- Oxygen in compound: -2 (usually)
- Hydrogen in compound: +1 (usually)

### Common Redox Reactions
- Metal + acid → salt + hydrogen (metal oxidized, H⁺ reduced)
- Metal + oxygen → metal oxide (metal oxidized)
- Displacement reactions

## 11. ELECTRICITY & CHEMISTRY

### Electrolysis
- Passing electric current through ionic compound (molten or aqueous)
- Decomposition by electricity

**Key terms:**
- **Anode:** Positive electrode (oxidation)
- **Cathode:** Negative electrode (reduction)
- **Electrolyte:** Ionic compound that conducts

### Products at Electrodes

**Molten electrolytes:**
- Metal cation → metal at cathode
- Non-metal anion → non-metal at anode

**Aqueous electrolytes:**
- Cathode: Less reactive metal (or H₂ if reactive metal like K, Na, Ca)
- Anode: Halide (Cl₂, Br₂, I₂) if present; otherwise O₂

**Examples:**
- Molten NaCl: Na at cathode, Cl₂ at anode
- Aqueous NaCl: H₂ at cathode, Cl₂ at anode, NaOH remains
- Aqueous CuSO₄: Cu at cathode, O₂ at anode

### Electroplating
- Object to be plated = cathode
- Metal to plate with = anode
- Electrolyte = salt of plating metal

## 12. ORGANIC CHEMISTRY

### Homologous Series

| Series | Functional Group | General Formula | Example |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Alkanes** | C-C single bonds | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | Methane CH₄ |
| **Alkenes** | C=C double bond | CₙH₂ₙ | Ethene C₂H₄ |
| **Alcohols** | -OH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | Ethanol C₂H₅OH |
| **Carboxylic acids** | -COOH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH | Ethanoic acid CH₃COOH |
| **Esters** | -COO- | — | Ethyl ethanoate |

### Alkanes
- Saturated (single bonds only)
- Unreactive (except combustion, substitution with UV)
- Combustion: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

### Alkenes
- Unsaturated (double bond)
- More reactive than alkanes
- **Test for unsaturation:** Add bromine water → decolourises (orange to colourless)
- Addition reactions: + H₂ (hydrogenation), + H₂O (hydration), + halogen

### Alcohols
- Combustion: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O
- Oxidation: Primary alcohol → aldehyde → carboxylic acid
- Fermentation: Glucose → ethanol + CO₂ (yeast, anaerobic)

### Carboxylic Acids
- Weak acids
- React with metals, bases, carbonates
- Esterification: Acid + Alcohol → Ester + Water (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst)

### Polymers

**Addition polymerization:**
- Alkenes join together
- No other product
- Examples: Poly(ethene), poly(chloroethene/PVC), poly(propene)

**Condensation polymerization:**
- Two different monomers
- Small molecule (usually water) eliminated
- Examples: Nylon, Terylene

### Isomerism
- Same molecular formula, different structure
- **Structural isomers:** Different arrangement of atoms
- Example: Butane (C₄H₁₀) and methylpropane

### Crude Oil
- Mixture of hydrocarbons
- Separated by fractional distillation
- Fractions: Refinery gas, gasoline, naphtha, kerosene, diesel, fuel oil, bitumen

## 13. METALS

### Reactivity Series

```
Potassium (K) — Most reactive
Sodium (Na)
Calcium (Ca)
Magnesium (Mg)
Aluminium (Al)
[Carbon]
Zinc (Zn)
Iron (Fe)
Lead (Pb)
[Hydrogen]
Copper (Cu)
Silver (Ag)
Gold (Au) — Least reactive
```

### Reactions of Metals
- With water/acid: More reactive → more vigorous
- Displacement: More reactive displaces less reactive from compound
- Reduction of oxides: Carbon can reduce metals below it in series

### Extraction of Metals
- **Electrolysis:** Very reactive metals (Al)
- **Reduction with carbon:** Moderately reactive (Fe, Zn)
- **Unreactive:** Found native (Au)

### Iron & Steel
- Iron from blast furnace: Iron ore + coke + limestone
- Steel = iron + carbon (0.5-1.5%)
- Stainless steel: Iron + chromium + nickel

### Rusting
- Iron + water + oxygen → hydrated iron(III) oxide
- Prevention: Painting, greasing, galvanizing, sacrificial protection

### Alloys
- Mixture of metal with other elements
- Properties often better than pure metal
- Examples: Brass (Cu + Zn), Bronze (Cu + Sn), Steel (Fe + C)

## 14. AIR & WATER

### Composition of Air
- ~78% nitrogen
- ~21% oxygen
- ~1% argon + other gases
- Variable water vapour + CO₂

### Fractional Distillation of Air
1. Filter to remove dust
2. Cool to remove CO₂ and water
3. Compress and cool to liquefy
4. Fractional distillation by boiling point

### Water Treatment
1. Screening (remove large solids)
2. Sedimentation (settling)
3. Filtration (sand beds)
4. Chlorination (kill bacteria)

### Tests for Water
- Cobalt chloride paper: Blue → pink
- Anhydrous copper(II) sulfate: White → blue

## 15. SULFUR & CARBON CYCLES

### Sulfur Cycle
- Volcanic activity releases SO₂
- Fossil fuels contain sulfur
- Burning produces SO₂ → acid rain
- Bacteria convert sulfur compounds

### Carbon Cycle
- Photosynthesis removes CO₂
- Respiration returns CO₂
- Combustion returns CO₂
- Decomposition returns CO₂

### Greenhouse Effect
- Gases trap heat in atmosphere
- Main gases: CO₂, methane (CH₄), water vapour
- Causes: Burning fossil fuels, deforestation, agriculture
- Effects: Global warming, climate change, sea level rise

## EXAM TIPS

1. **Write balanced equations** — Check atom counts on both sides
2. **State symbols:** (s), (l), (g), (aq) — use them
3. **Learn reactivity series** — Essential for predictions
4. **Know ion tests** — Flame colours, precipitates, gases
5. **Mole calculations:** Show working — moles → mass/volume
6. **Periodic trends:** Group 1 down = more reactive; Group 7 down = less reactive
7. **Organic chemistry:** Learn functional groups and tests
8. **Redox:** Remember OIL RIG
9. **Equilibrium:** Le Chatelier — system opposes change
10. **Read question twice** — Don't miss "state," "explain," or "give reasons"
