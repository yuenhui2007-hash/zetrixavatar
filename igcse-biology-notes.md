## 2. CELLS

### Cell Structure

**Animal Cell:**
- Nucleus — controls cell activities, contains DNA
- Cytoplasm — chemical reactions occur
- Cell membrane — controls entry/exit
- Mitochondria — aerobic respiration
- Ribosomes — protein synthesis

**Plant Cell (additional features):**
- Cell wall — cellulose, support
- Chloroplasts — photosynthesis
- Permanent vacuole — sap storage, turgidity

**Bacterial Cell (prokaryote):**
- No nucleus — DNA in cytoplasm (loop)
- Cell wall — not cellulose
- May have flagella, capsule, plasmids

### Levels of Organization
```
Cell → Tissue → Organ → Organ System → Organism
```

**Examples:**
- Tissue: Muscle tissue, xylem tissue
- Organ: Heart, leaf, stomach
- System: Circulatory system, digestive system

## 4. ENZYMES

### Characteristics
- **Biological catalysts** — speed up reactions without being used up
- **Specific** — one enzyme, one reaction (lock and key)
- **Affected by temperature and pH**

### Lock and Key Model
- Enzyme = lock
- Substrate = key
- Active site = where substrate fits
- Products released, enzyme unchanged

### Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

**Temperature:**
- Low temp: Slow movement, few collisions
- Optimum: Maximum rate
- High temp: Enzyme denatures (active site changes shape)

**pH:**
- Each enzyme has optimum pH
- Too acidic/alkaline: Denaturation

### Enzyme Uses
- **Biological washing powder:** Proteases, lipases remove stains
- **Food industry:** Pectinase in fruit juice, rennin in cheese
- **Baby food:** Pre-digest proteins

## 6. TRANSPORT IN ANIMALS

### Blood Components

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Red blood cells** | Carry oxygen (haemoglobin) |
| **White blood cells** | Fight infection (phagocytosis, antibodies) |
| **Platelets** | Blood clotting |
| **Plasma** | Transport (nutrients, hormones, waste, CO₂) |

### Heart Structure
- **Right atrium** → **Right ventricle** → Lungs (pulmonary artery)
- **Left atrium** → **Left ventricle** → Body (aorta)
- Valves prevent backflow
- Left ventricle wall thicker (pumps further)

### Double Circulation
1. **Pulmonary:** Heart → Lungs → Heart
2. **Systemic:** Heart → Body → Heart

### Blood Vessels

| Vessel | Wall | Lumen | Valve | Blood Flow |
|--------|------|-------|-------|-----------|
| **Artery** | Thick, muscular | Small | No | Away from heart |
| **Vein** | Thin | Large | Yes | To heart |
| **Capillary** | One cell thick | Very small | No | Exchange |

## 7. TRANSPORT IN PLANTS

### Xylem
- Transports water + minerals
- Dead cells, lignified walls
- Transpiration stream
- Root pressure + capillary action + transpiration pull

### Phloem
- Transports sucrose + amino acids
- Living cells (sieve tubes + companion cells)
- Translocation (source to sink)

### Transpiration
- Evaporation of water from leaf surface
- Stomata control water loss
- Factors affecting rate: Temperature, humidity, wind speed, light intensity

### Root Hair Cells
- Large surface area for water absorption
- Active transport of mineral ions

## 8. RESPIRATION

### Aerobic Respiration
```
Glucose + Oxygen → Carbon Dioxide + Water + Energy
C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP
```
- Occurs in mitochondria
- Releases more energy than anaerobic

### Anaerobic Respiration

**In animals:**
```
Glucose → Lactic Acid + Energy
```
- Oxygen debt → rapid breathing to repay

**In yeast:**
```
Glucose → Ethanol + Carbon Dioxide + Energy
```
- Fermentation (used in brewing, baking)

### Comparing Respiration

| Feature | Aerobic | Anaerobic |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Oxygen needed | Yes | No |
| Energy released | Much (38 ATP) | Little (2 ATP) |
| Products | CO₂ + H₂O | Lactic acid / ethanol + CO₂ |
| Location | Mitochondria | Cytoplasm |

## 9. GAS EXCHANGE

### Breathing System

| Structure | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Nose/mouth** | Air entry, warming, filtering |
| **Trachea** | Windpipe, supported by cartilage |
| **Bronchi/bronchioles** | Branching airways |
| **Alveoli** | Gas exchange site |
| **Diaphragm** | Breathing muscle |

### Alveoli Adaptations
- Large surface area (millions)
- Thin walls (one cell)
- Good blood supply
- Moist surface

### Breathing Mechanism
**Inhalation:** Diaphragm contracts (flattens), ribcage up/out → volume up → pressure down → air in
**Exhalation:** Diaphragm relaxes, ribcage down/in → volume down → pressure up → air out

### Effects of Smoking
- Tar → lung cancer, bronchitis
- Nicotine → addiction, heart disease
- Carbon monoxide → reduces oxygen transport

## 10. EXCRETION

### Kidney Function
- Filters blood
- Reabsorbs useful substances
- Produces urine

### Nephron Structure
- **Glomerulus:** Filters blood (high pressure)
- **Bowman's capsule:** Collects filtrate
- **Proximal tubule:** Reabsorbs glucose, amino acids
- **Loop of Henle:** Concentrates urine
- **Distal tubule:** Fine tuning
- **Collecting duct:** Water reabsorption (ADH)

### ADH (Antidiuretic Hormone)
- Released when blood concentrated
- Makes collecting duct more permeable
- More water reabsorbed → concentrated urine

### Dialysis
- Artificial kidney function
- Blood filtered through partially permeable membrane
- Removes urea, excess salts, water

## 11. COORDINATION & RESPONSE

### Nervous System
- **Central:** Brain + spinal cord
- **Peripheral:** Nerves

### Neurone Types
- **Sensory:** Receptor to CNS
- **Relay:** Within CNS
- **Motor:** CNS to effector

### Reflex Arc
```
Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory neurone → Relay neurone → Motor neurone → Effector → Response
```
- Faster than conscious thought
- Protective (e.g., pulling hand from hot object)

### Eye Structure
- **Cornea:** Refracts light
- **Iris:** Controls pupil size
- **Lens:** Focuses light
- **Retina:** Contains receptors (rods + cones)
- **Optic nerve:** Sends signals to brain

**Accommodation:**
- Near: Ciliary muscles contract, lens thickens
- Far: Ciliary muscles relax, lens flattens

### Hormones

| Hormone | Gland | Effect |
|---------|-------|--------|
| **Insulin** | Pancreas | Lowers blood glucose |
| **Glucagon** | Pancreas | Raises blood glucose |
| **ADH** | Pituitary | Water reabsorption |
| **Growth hormone** | Pituitary | Growth |
| **Testosterone** | Testes | Male characteristics |
| **Oestrogen** | Ovaries | Female characteristics |
| **Progesterone** | Ovaries | Maintains pregnancy |

### Homeostasis
- Maintaining constant internal environment
- Negative feedback: Change detected → response opposes change

## 12. REPRODUCTION

### Human Reproductive System

**Male:**
- Testes: Produce sperm + testosterone
- Sperm ducts: Transport sperm
- Prostate gland: Adds fluid
- Penis: Delivery

**Female:**
- Ovaries: Produce eggs + hormones
- Oviducts: Fertilization site
- Uterus: Development site
- Cervix: Entrance to uterus
- Vagina: Birth canal

### Menstrual Cycle
- **Day 1-5:** Menstruation (lining sheds)
- **Day 6-14:** Follicle develops, oestrogen rises, lining rebuilds
- **Day 14:** Ovulation (egg released)
- **Day 15-28:** Corpus luteum produces progesterone, lining maintained

### Fertilization to Birth
1. **Fertilization:** Sperm + egg in oviduct → zygote
2. **Implantation:** Zygote divides, embeds in uterus wall
3. **Embryo:** 2-8 weeks, organs form
4. **Fetus:** 8 weeks to birth
5. **Placenta:** Exchange (food, O₂, waste, CO₂)
6. **Birth:** Cervix dilates, contractions, baby + afterbirth delivered

### Plant Reproduction

**Flower structure:**
- Sepals: Protect bud
- Petals: Attract insects
- Stamens: Male parts (anther + filament)
- Carpels: Female parts (stigma, style, ovary)

**Pollination:** Transfer of pollen to stigma
- Self-pollination: Same flower
- Cross-pollination: Different flower

**Fertilization:** Pollen tube grows to ovule, fuses with egg cell

**Seed dispersal:** Wind, water, animals, explosion

## 13. INHERITANCE

### Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Gene** | Section of DNA controlling characteristic |
| **Allele** | Alternative form of gene |
| **Genotype** | Genetic makeup (e.g., Tt) |
| **Phenotype** | Observable characteristic |
| **Homozygous** | Two identical alleles (TT or tt) |
| **Heterozygous** | Two different alleles (Tt) |
| **Dominant** | Allele always expressed |
| **Recessive** | Allele only expressed if homozygous |

### Genetic Diagrams
- Use Punnett squares
- Show parental genotypes, gametes, offspring genotypes + phenotypes
- Express as ratios

### Monohybrid Cross
- Single gene inheritance
- Example: Tall (T) vs dwarf (t) plants

### Sex Determination
- Females: XX
- Males: XY
- Father determines sex (X or Y sperm)

### Genetic Disorders

| Disorder | Cause | Inheritance |
|----------|-------|-------------|
| **Sickle cell anaemia** | Faulty haemoglobin gene | Recessive |
| **Cystic fibrosis** | Faulty CFTR gene | Recessive |
| **Huntington's disease** | Faulty huntingtin gene | Dominant |

### Variation
- **Continuous:** Range (e.g., height, weight) — polygenic + environment
- **Discontinuous:** Distinct categories (e.g., blood group) — single gene

### Selective Breeding
- Choose parents with desired characteristics
- Example: Cattle for milk yield, wheat for disease resistance

### Genetic Engineering
1. Identify desired gene
2. Cut out using enzymes (restriction enzymes)
3. Insert into vector (plasmid)
4. Transfer to target organism
5. Verify expression

**Examples:** Insulin production, GM crops

## 14. ECOSYSTEMS & ENVIRONMENT

### Food Chains
```
Producer → Primary Consumer → Secondary Consumer → Tertiary Consumer
(Plant)    (Herbivore)        (Carnivore)          (Top carnivore)
```

### Energy Flow
- Sun → Producers (photosynthesis)
- 90% energy lost at each trophic level
- Lost as: Heat, movement, undigested, excretion

### Pyramids
- **Pyramid of numbers:** Organisms at each level
- **Pyramid of biomass:** Dry mass at each level
- **Pyramid of energy:** Energy at each level

### Carbon Cycle
1. CO₂ in atmosphere
2. Photosynthesis removes CO₂
3. Respiration returns CO₂
4. Decomposition returns CO₂
5. Combustion returns CO₂

### Nitrogen Cycle
- Nitrogen fixation (bacteria in soil/legume roots)
- Nitrification (bacteria convert ammonia → nitrates)
- Denitrification (bacteria return N₂ to atmosphere)
- Plants absorb nitrates

### Water Cycle
- Evaporation → Transpiration → Condensation → Precipitation → Collection

### Pollution
- **Eutrophication:** Fertilizers → algal bloom → oxygen depletion → fish death
- **Acid rain:** SO₂/NOx → acidifies water/soil
- **Deforestation:** Habitat loss, CO₂ increase, soil erosion

### Conservation
- Sustainable use of resources
- Methods: Protected areas, breeding programmes, education, sustainable fishing

## EXAM TIPS

1. **Use precise terminology** — "photosynthesis" not "plants making food"
2. **Label diagrams clearly** — Use straight lines, correct spelling
3. **Explain "why" not just "what"** — Link structure to function
4. **Show working in genetics** — Punnett squares with clear labels
5. **Use data from graphs** — Quote values, describe trends
6. **Compare with "whereas"** — Highlight differences
7. **State the obvious** — Even basic points earn marks
8. **Check units** — mm, cm, μm, mg, g, kg
9. **Read the command word** — State, describe, explain, compare, evaluate
10. **Leave time for last questions** — They often have high marks
