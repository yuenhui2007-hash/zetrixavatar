# IGCSE Economics Notes (0455)

## 1. THE BASIC ECONOMIC PROBLEM

### Scarcity
> Unlimited wants + Limited resources = Need to make choices

### Factors of Production

| Factor | Description | Reward |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Land** | Natural resources | Rent |
| **Labour** | Human effort | Wages |
| **Capital** | Man-made tools, machinery | Interest |
| **Enterprise** | Risk-taking, organizing | Profit |

### Opportunity Cost
> The next best alternative foregone when a choice is made.

### Production Possibility Curve (PPC)
- Shows maximum output combinations of two goods
- Points on curve: Efficient
- Points inside: Inefficient
- Points outside: Unattainable (with current resources)
- Outward shift: Economic growth

### Economic Systems

| System | What? | How? | For whom? |
|--------|-------|------|-----------|
| **Free market** | Consumer choice | Private enterprise | Ability to pay |
| **Planned** | Government decides | State-owned | Government priority |
| **Mixed** | Both | Both | Both |

## 2. THE ALLOCATION OF RESOURCES

### Demand
- Quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at various prices
- **Law of demand:** Price ↑ → Quantity demanded ↓

### Supply
- Quantity producers are willing and able to sell at various prices
- **Law of supply:** Price ↑ → Quantity supplied ↑

### Determinants of Demand
1. Income
2. Prices of substitutes
3. Prices of complements
4. Tastes/preferences
5. Population
6. Expectations

### Determinants of Supply
1. Costs of production
2. Technology
3. Number of producers
4. Taxes/subsidies
5. Expectations

### Equilibrium
- Where demand = supply
- Market clears (no surplus/shortage)

### Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

```
PED = (% change in quantity demanded) / (% change in price)
```

| Value | Elasticity | Effect on revenue if price rises |
|-------|-----------|--------------------------------|
| PED > 1 | Elastic | Revenue falls |
| PED = 1 | Unit elastic | Revenue unchanged |
| PED < 1 | Inelastic | Revenue rises |

**Factors affecting PED:**
- Availability of substitutes
- Necessity vs luxury
- Proportion of income
- Time period

### Cross Elasticity of Demand (XED)
```
XED = (% change in demand for A) / (% change in price of B)
```
- XED > 0: Substitutes
- XED < 0: Complements

### Income Elasticity of Demand (YED)
```
YED = (% change in demand) / (% change in income)
```
- YED > 0: Normal good
- YED < 0: Inferior good

### Price Elasticity of Supply (PES)
- PES > 1: Elastic
- PES < 1: Inelastic

### Consumer Surplus
- Difference between what consumers are willing to pay and what they actually pay
- Area below demand curve, above price

### Producer Surplus
- Difference between what producers receive and minimum they would accept
- Area above supply curve, below price

## 3. MICROECONOMIC DECISION MAKERS

### Money & Banking

**Functions of money:**
1. Medium of exchange
2. Measure of value
3. Store of value
4. Standard for deferred payment

**Central bank functions:**
- Issue currency
- Banker to government
- Banker to commercial banks
- Lender of last resort
- Monetary policy

**Commercial banks:**
- Accept deposits
- Make loans
- Transfer money

### Households
- Suppliers of factors of production
- Consumers of goods/services
- Save or spend income

### Workers
- **Specialization:** Focus on one task → efficiency
- **Division of labour:** Breaking production into tasks
- **Trade unions:** Bargain for workers' interests

### Firms
- Organize production
- Aim to maximize profit (usually)
- Types: Sole trader, partnership, Ltd, PLC

### Governments
- Provide public goods
- Redistribute income
- Correct market failures
- Stabilize economy

## 4. GOVERNMENT & THE MACROECONOMY

### Government Economic Objectives

| Objective | Target |
|-----------|--------|
| **Full employment** | Low unemployment |
| **Price stability** | Low inflation |
| **Economic growth** | Rising real GDP |
| **Balance of payments** | Sustainable current account |
| **Redistribution of income** | Reduced inequality |

### Fiscal Policy
- Government use of taxation and spending
- **Expansionary:** Lower taxes, increase spending → boost demand
- **Contractionary:** Raise taxes, cut spending → reduce demand

### Monetary Policy
- Control of interest rates and money supply
- **Lower interest rates:** Encourage borrowing/spending/investment
- **Higher interest rates:** Discourage borrowing, encourage saving

### Supply-Side Policy
- Policies to increase productive capacity
- Education, training, privatization, deregulation

## 5. ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT

### Living Standards
- **GDP:** Total value of goods/services produced
- **GDP per capita:** GDP / population
- **Limitations of GDP:** Doesn't measure inequality, environment, leisure, informal economy

### Poverty
- **Absolute poverty:** Below minimum income for basic needs
- **Relative poverty:** Below average income in society

### Population

| Factor | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Birth rate** | Number of live births per 1000 population |
| **Death rate** | Number of deaths per 1000 population |
| **Natural increase** | Birth rate - Death rate |
| **Net migration** | Immigrants - Emigrants |
| **Population growth** | Natural increase + Net migration |

### Developing vs Developed Economies

| Feature | Developing | Developed |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| GDP per capita | Low | High |
| Life expectancy | Lower | Higher |
| Literacy | Lower | Higher |
| Infant mortality | Higher | Lower |
| Industry | Primary dominant | Tertiary dominant |
| Birth rate | Higher | Lower |

### Measures to Promote Development
- Education and training
- Healthcare improvements
- Infrastructure investment
- Foreign aid
- Foreign direct investment (FDI)
- Microfinance
- Fair trade
- Debt relief

## 6. INTERNATIONAL TRADE

### Why Trade?
- Countries have different resources
- Specialization increases efficiency
- Economies of scale
- Greater choice for consumers
- Lower prices

### Absolute vs Comparative Advantage
- **Absolute advantage:** Can produce more with same resources
- **Comparative advantage:** Lower opportunity cost

### Protectionism

| Method | How it works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Tariffs** | Tax on imports |
| **Quotas** | Limit on quantity |
| **Subsidies** | Government support to domestic producers |
| **Embargoes** | Complete ban |

**Arguments for protectionism:**
- Protect infant industries
- Protect jobs
- National security
- Prevent dumping

**Arguments against protectionism:**
- Higher prices for consumers
- Less choice
- Retaliation
- Inefficiency

### Free Trade Areas
- Countries agree to reduce trade barriers
- Examples: EU (single market), ASEAN, USMCA

### Exchange Rates
- **Strong currency:** Imports cheaper, exports expensive
- **Weak currency:** Imports expensive, exports cheaper

### Balance of Payments

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Current account** | Trade in goods/services, income, transfers |
| **Capital account** | Capital transfers |
| **Financial account** | Investment flows |

**Current account deficit:** Importing more than exporting
**Current account surplus:** Exporting more than importing

## 7. MARKET FAILURE

### Types of Market Failure

#### Externalities
- Costs or benefits affecting third parties

| Type | Effect |
|------|--------|
| **Negative production** | Pollution from factory |
| **Negative consumption** | Smoking, congestion |
| **Positive production** | Beekeeping (pollination) |
| **Positive consumption** | Vaccinations, education |

**Government solutions:**
- Taxes (Pigouvian tax)
- Subsidies
- Regulation
- Tradable permits

#### Public Goods
- Non-excludable and non-rivalrous
- Free-rider problem
- Examples: Defence, street lighting
- Government must provide

#### Merit & Demerit Goods

| | Merit Goods | Demerit Goods |
|---|-------------|---------------|
| **Definition** | Socially beneficial, under-consumed | Socially harmful, over-consumed |
| **Examples** | Education, healthcare | Cigarettes, alcohol |
| **Problem** | Information failure | Information failure |
| **Government action** | Subsidies, free provision | Taxes, bans, education |

## 8. PRODUCTION

### Costs of Production

| Cost | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Fixed costs** | Don't vary with output (rent) |
| **Variable costs** | Vary with output (raw materials) |
| **Total cost** | Fixed + Variable |
| **Average cost** | Total cost / Output |
| **Marginal cost** | Cost of producing one more unit |

### Revenue

| Revenue | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Total revenue** | Price × Quantity |
| **Average revenue** | Total revenue / Quantity = Price |

### Profit
```
Profit = Total revenue - Total cost
```

### Economies of Scale
- Cost advantages from large-scale production

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Purchasing** | Bulk buying discounts |
| **Technical** | Efficient machinery |
| **Financial** | Cheaper borrowing |
| **Managerial** | Specialization |
| **Marketing** | Spreading fixed costs |

### Diseconomies of Scale
- Cost disadvantages from being too large
- Communication problems, coordination issues, worker alienation

## 9. MARKET STRUCTURE

### Perfect Competition
- Many buyers and sellers
- Homogeneous products
- No barriers to entry
- Price takers
- Normal profit in long run

### Monopoly
- Single seller
- High barriers to entry
- Price maker
- Can make supernormal profit

### Oligopoly
- Few dominant firms
- Interdependence
- Non-price competition (advertising, branding)
- Barriers to entry

### Monopolistic Competition
- Many firms
- Differentiated products
- Some price control
- Low barriers

## 10. INFLATION & UNEMPLOYMENT

### Inflation
- Sustained increase in general price level
- Measured by CPI (Consumer Price Index)

**Causes:**
- Demand-pull (too much demand)
- Cost-push (rising costs)
- Monetary (too much money)

**Effects:**
- Reduced purchasing power
- Menu costs
- Uncertainty
- Redistribution (debtors gain, creditors lose)

### Unemployment

| Type | Cause |
|------|-------|
| **Frictional** | Time between jobs |
| **Structural** | Skills/location mismatch |
| **Cyclical** | Economic downturn |
| **Seasonal** | Seasonal industries |

**Effects:**
- Lost output
- Government cost (benefits)
- Social problems

## EXAM TIPS

1. **Define key terms** — Start with clear definitions
2. **Use diagrams** — Demand/supply shifts, PPC, externalities diagrams
3. **Explain with examples** — Real-world applications
4. **Evaluate** — Consider both sides, use "however"
5. **Distinguish between** — Similar concepts (e.g., shift vs movement along curve)
6. **Calculate carefully** — Percentage changes, elasticity
7. **Apply to context** — Use case study data
8. **Command words:**
   - **Define:** Give meaning
   - **Describe:** State what happens
   - **Explain:** Give reasons why
   - **Analyze:** Break down into parts
   - **Evaluate:** Consider strengths and weaknesses
   - **Discuss:** Examine from different viewpoints
