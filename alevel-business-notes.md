# A-Level Business Studies Notes

## 1. BUSINESS & ITS ENVIRONMENT

### What is Business?
> An organization or enterprising entity engaged in commercial, industrial, or professional activities.

### Types of Business Entities

| Type | Ownership | Liability | Control | Examples |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------|----------|
| **Sole Trader** | One person | Unlimited | Full | Local shop, freelancer |
| **Partnership** | 2+ people | Unlimited (usually) | Shared | Law firm, dental practice |
| **Private Limited Company (Ltd)** | Shareholders | Limited | Board + Shareholders | Family businesses |
| **Public Limited Company (PLC)** | Public shareholders | Limited | Board + AGM | Tesco, BP |
| **Social Enterprise** | Various | Various | Stakeholders | Charity shops, co-ops |
| **Franchise** | Franchisee owns outlet | Limited | Franchisor sets rules | McDonald's, Subway |

### Business Objectives
- **Survival** — short term, new businesses
- **Profit maximization** — traditional assumption
- **Revenue maximization** — market share focus
- **Sales maximization** — growth focus
- **Satisficing** — satisfactory rather than optimal (behavioral theory)
- **Corporate social responsibility (CSR)** — stakeholder focus
- **Growth** — internal/external expansion

### Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Interests | Conflict |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| **Shareholders** | Dividends, share price | vs Employees (wages) |
| **Employees** | Wages, conditions, security | vs Shareholders (costs) |
| **Customers** | Quality, price, service | vs Business (profit margins) |
| **Suppliers** | Prompt payment, orders | vs Business (lower prices) |
| **Government** | Tax, employment, regulation | vs Business (compliance costs) |
| **Local Community** | Jobs, environment | vs Business (pollution) |

## 2. EXTERNAL ENVIRONMENT

### PESTLE Analysis

| Factor | Examples |
|--------|----------|
| **Political** | Tax policy, trade restrictions, political stability |
| **Economic** | Interest rates, inflation, exchange rates, growth |
| **Social** | Demographics, education, lifestyle changes, attitudes |
| **Technological** | Automation, R&D, innovation, disruption |
| **Legal** | Employment law, consumer protection, health & safety |
| **Environmental** | Climate, sustainability regulations, carbon taxes |

### SWOT Analysis
- **Strengths** — Internal, positive
- **Weaknesses** — Internal, negative
- **Opportunities** — External, positive
- **Threats** — External, negative

### Porter's Five Forces
1. **Threat of New Entrants** — Barriers to entry (capital, regulations, brand loyalty)
2. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers** — Few suppliers, unique inputs
3. **Bargaining Power of Buyers** — Many alternatives, low switching costs
4. **Threat of Substitutes** — Alternative products/services
5. **Competitive Rivalry** — Number and strength of competitors

## 3. MARKETING

### Marketing Mix (7Ps)

| P | Description | Key Decisions |
|---|-------------|---------------|
| **Product** | Goods/services offered | Design, features, quality, branding, packaging, USP |
| **Price** | Amount charged | Cost-plus, penetration, skimming, psychological, competitive |
| **Place** | Distribution channels | Retailers, online, direct, wholesalers, agents |
| **Promotion** | Communication with customers | Advertising, PR, sales promotion, personal selling, digital |
| **People** | Staff involved | Training, appearance, customer service |
| **Process** | Delivery method | Efficiency, queues, systems, technology |
| **Physical Evidence** | Tangible cues | Environment, packaging, website, testimonials |

### Product Life Cycle

| Stage | Sales | Profit | Strategy |
|-------|-------|--------|----------|
| **Introduction** | Low | Negative | Inform, skimming/penetration pricing |
| **Growth** | Rapid | Rising | Build brand, expand distribution |
| **Maturity** | Peak | High | Defend share, differentiate, extend |
| **Decline** | Falling | Falling | Harvest, divest, rebrand |

### Pricing Strategies
- **Penetration:** Low price to gain market share
- **Price Skimming:** High price initially for early adopters
- **Cost-Plus:** Cost + markup percentage
- **Competitive:** Match/average of competitors
- **Psychological:** $9.99 instead of $10
- **Premium:** High price signals quality
- **Predatory:** Below cost to eliminate competition (illegal)

### Market Research

| Primary Research | Secondary Research |
|------------------|--------------------|
| Surveys, interviews, focus groups | Government stats, industry reports, internet |
| Expensive, time-consuming, specific | Cheaper, faster, may be outdated |
| First-hand data | Already exists |

**Sampling Methods:**
- Random — everyone equal chance
- Stratified — proportional subgroups
- Quota — specific numbers per segment
- Convenience — easiest to reach

## 4. OPERATIONS MANAGEMENT

### Operations Objectives
- Cost minimization
- Quality assurance
- Speed/lead time reduction
- Flexibility
- Dependability/reliability
- Sustainability

### Methods of Production

| Method | Description | Best For |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Job Production** | One-off, customized | Wedding cakes, bespoke furniture |
| **Batch Production** | Groups of similar items | Bakeries, clothing sizes |
| **Flow/Mass Production** | Continuous, standardized | Cars, electronics |
| **Cell Production** | Teams responsible for units | Assembly lines |

### Lean Production
- **JIT (Just In Time):** Materials arrive when needed — reduces waste, lowers inventory
- **Kaizen:** Continuous improvement
- **Andon:** Stop production when defect found
- **Jidoka:** Automation with human oversight
- **TQM (Total Quality Management):** Quality at every stage
- **Zero Defects:** Aim for perfection

### Quality Control vs Quality Assurance

| Quality Control | Quality Assurance |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Inspect at end | Build in throughout |
| Reactive | Proactive |
| Fault finding | Fault prevention |
| Higher waste | Lower waste |

### Capacity Utilization
```
Capacity Utilization = (Actual Output / Maximum Possible Output) × 100
```
- **Under-utilization:** Wasted fixed costs, inefficiency
- **Over-utilization:** Breakdowns, quality issues, staff burnout
- **Ways to improve:** Subcontracting, shift work, new machinery

## 5. FINANCE & ACCOUNTING

### Sources of Finance

| Short-Term | Medium/Long-Term |
|------------|------------------|
| Overdraft | Bank loans |
| Trade credit | Share capital |
| Factoring | Debentures |
| Leasing | Retained profits |
| | Venture capital |

**Internal vs External:**

### Financial Statements
- **Income Statement:** Revenue, costs, profit/loss
- **Statement of Financial Position:** Assets, liabilities, equity
- **Cash Flow Statement:** Cash inflows/outflows

### Ratio Analysis
(See Accounting notes for detailed ratio formulas)

| Category | Key Ratios |
|----------|-----------|
| **Profitability** | Gross profit margin, net profit margin, ROCE |
| **Liquidity** | Current ratio, acid test |
| **Efficiency** | Inventory turnover, receivables days, payables days |
| **Gearing** | Debt/equity, interest cover |
| **Investor** | EPS, dividend yield, P/E ratio |

### Break-Even Analysis
```
Break-even (units) = Fixed Costs / (Selling Price - Variable Cost per unit)
Break-even ($) = Fixed Costs / Contribution/Sales Ratio
```

## 6. HUMAN RESOURCE MANAGEMENT

### Workforce Planning
- Forecasting labour needs
- Recruitment and selection
- Training and development
- Performance appraisal

### Recruitment Process
1. Job analysis → Job description + Person specification
2. Advertise vacancy
3. Screen applications/CVs
4. Interview/assessment
5. Select and appoint
6. Induction

### Training Methods

| On-the-Job | Off-the-Job |
|------------|-------------|
| Coaching, mentoring, job rotation | Courses, seminars, simulations |
| Cheaper, immediate application | Higher quality, broader perspective |
| May pick up bad habits | More expensive, time away |

### Motivation Theories

#### Taylor — Scientific Management
- Workers motivated by money
- Time-and-motion studies
- Piece-rate pay

#### Maslow — Hierarchy of Needs
```
Self-actualization
Esteem
Social/Belonging
Safety/Security
Physiological
```

#### Herzberg — Two-Factor Theory
- **Motivators:** Achievement, recognition, responsibility, advancement
- **Hygiene Factors:** Salary, conditions, job security, company policy

#### McGregor — Theory X & Y
- **Theory X:** Workers lazy, need control (authoritarian)
- **Theory Y:** Workers motivated, seek responsibility (participative)

#### Adams — Equity Theory
- Workers compare their input/output ratio to others
- Inequity causes demotivation

### Leadership Styles

| Style | Characteristics | When Effective |
|-------|----------------|---------------|
| **Autocratic** | Dictatorial, top-down | Crisis, unskilled workers |
| **Democratic** | Consultative, participative | Skilled workforce, innovation needed |
| **Laissez-faire** | Hands-off, delegating | Experts, self-motivated teams |
| **Paternalistic** | Caring but controlling | Traditional cultures |
| **Transformational** | Inspirational, visionary | Change management |
| **Transactional** | Reward/punishment based | Routine tasks |

### Organizational Structure

| Type | Features | Pros | Cons |
|------|----------|------|------|
| **Flat** | Few hierarchy levels | Fast communication, empowerment | Wide span of control |
| **Tall** | Many hierarchy levels | Clear roles, close supervision | Slow communication |
| **Matrix** | Dual reporting | Flexibility, resource sharing | Conflicting loyalties |
| **Hierarchical** | Traditional pyramid | Clear authority | Bureaucratic |

## 7. STRATEGIC MANAGEMENT

### Ansoff Matrix

| | Existing Products | New Products |
|---|------------------|-------------|
| **Existing Markets** | Market Penetration | Product Development |
| **New Markets** | Market Development | Diversification |

### Growth Strategies
- **Organic/Internal:** Using own resources
- **External:** Mergers, acquisitions, joint ventures
- **Horizontal Integration:** Same industry, same stage
- **Vertical Integration:** Same industry, different stage (forward/backward)
- **Conglomerate Integration:** Different industries

### Decision-Making Techniques

| Technique | Use |
|-----------|-----|
| **Decision Trees** | Visualize choices, outcomes, probabilities |
| **Investment Appraisal** | NPV, ARR, Payback, IRR |
| **Force Field Analysis** | For/against change |
| **Critical Path Analysis** | Project planning |

### Investment Appraisal

| Method | Formula/Approach | Decision Rule |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|
| **Payback Period** | Time to recover initial cost | Shorter is better |
| **Accounting Rate of Return (ARR)** | Average profit / Average investment | Higher is better |
| **Net Present Value (NPV)** | Sum of discounted cash flows | Positive = accept |
| **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** | Discount rate where NPV = 0 | Higher than cost of capital |

> **NPV is theoretically the best method** — accounts for time value of money and absolute return.

### Critical Path Analysis (CPA)
- **Activities:** Tasks with duration
- **Nodes:** Points where activities start/end
- **Critical Path:** Longest path — determines minimum project duration
- **Float/Slack:** Time an activity can be delayed without delaying project

## 9. GLOBALIZATION & INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS

### Reasons for Globalization
- Trade liberalization
- Technology/communication advances
- Transport improvements
- MNC growth
- Economic interdependence

### Methods of Entry

| Method | Risk/Control | Example |
|--------|-------------|---------|
| **Exporting** | Low risk, low control | Selling to distributor |
| **Licensing/Franchising** | Medium risk, medium control | McDonald's franchise |
| **Joint Venture** | Shared risk, shared control | Local + foreign firm |
| **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)** | High risk, high control | Building factory abroad |

### Exchange Rate Impact
- **Strong domestic currency:** Cheaper imports, expensive exports
- **Weak domestic currency:** Expensive imports, cheaper exports

## 10. CORPORATE SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY (CSR)

### CSR Areas
- **Environmental:** Carbon footprint, waste reduction, sustainable sourcing
- **Social:** Fair wages, community investment, diversity
- **Economic:** Profitable but ethical, tax compliance
- **Philanthropic:** Charitable donations, volunteering

### Benefits of CSR
- Enhanced brand reputation
- Customer loyalty
- Employee recruitment/retention
- Risk reduction
- Potential cost savings
- Investor appeal

### Tension with Profit
- Short-term cost vs long-term benefit
- Stakeholder vs shareholder primacy
- Friedman: "Social responsibility of business is to increase profits"

## EXAM TIPS

1. **Use frameworks** — Apply PESTLE, SWOT, Ansoff, Porter's 5 Forces to case studies
2. **Quantitative analysis** — Calculate ratios, break-even, investment appraisal
3. **Chain of reasoning** — Explain how and why, not just what
4. **Evaluate** — Always consider "it depends on...", short vs long term, stakeholder perspectives
5. **Context** — Apply to the specific business in the case study
6. **Real examples** — Support with known businesses
7. **Balanced argument** — Pros AND cons
8. **Recommend** — State what the business should do and justify
