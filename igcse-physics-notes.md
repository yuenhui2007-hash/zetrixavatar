# IGCSE Physics Notes (0625)

## 1. FORCES & MOTION

### Scalar vs Vector Quantities

| Scalar | Vector |
|--------|--------|
| Speed | Velocity |
| Distance | Displacement |
| Mass | Force |
| Time | Acceleration |
| Energy | Weight |
| Temperature | Momentum |

### Speed, Velocity & Acceleration

```
Speed = Distance / Time
Velocity = Displacement / Time
Acceleration = Change in velocity / Time
         a = (v - u) / t
```

### Equations of Motion (SUVAT)
```
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
s = ½(u + v)t
v² = u² + 2as
```

### Distance-Time Graphs
- Gradient = speed
- Flat section = stationary
- Steeper gradient = faster
- Curved = changing speed

### Speed-Time / Velocity-Time Graphs
- Gradient = acceleration
- Flat section = constant speed
- Area under graph = distance travelled

### Terminal Velocity
- Object falling through fluid
- Initially accelerates (weight > air resistance)
- Eventually air resistance = weight
- Reaches constant (terminal) velocity
- Example: Skydiver, falling raindrop

### Newton's Laws

**First Law:**
> Object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by resultant force.

**Second Law:**
```
F = ma
Resultant force = mass × acceleration
```

**Third Law:**
> Every action has equal and opposite reaction.

### Mass vs Weight

| Mass | Weight |
|------|--------|
| Amount of matter | Force of gravity |
| kg | N |
| Same everywhere | Changes with gravity |

```
W = mg
Weight = mass × gravitational field strength (g ≈ 10 N/kg)
```

## 2. FORCES

### Types of Forces
- **Contact forces:** Friction, air resistance, tension, normal contact force
- **Non-contact forces:** Gravitational, magnetic, electrostatic

### Friction
- Opposes motion
- Causes heating
- Can be useful (brakes) or a problem (wasted energy)

### Hooke's Law
```
F = kx
Force = spring constant × extension
```
- Valid up to limit of proportionality
- Graph of force vs extension is straight line through origin

### Circular Motion
- Constant speed, changing direction
- Resultant force towards centre (centripetal force)
- Without force, object moves in straight line (Newton's 1st law)

---

## 3. MOMENTUM

### Definition
```
p = mv
Momentum = mass × velocity
```
- Vector quantity (direction matters)
- Unit: kg m/s

### Conservation of Momentum
> Total momentum before = Total momentum after (in closed system)

### Impulse
```
Impulse = Force × Time = Change in momentum
F × t = m(v - u)
```
- Safety features increase time to reduce force (crumple zones, airbags)

### Collisions

| Type | Kinetic Energy | Momentum |
|------|---------------|----------|
| **Elastic** | Conserved | Conserved |
| **Inelastic** | Not conserved | Conserved |

---

## 4. TURNING EFFECTS

### Moment of a Force
```
Moment = Force × Perpendicular distance from pivot
```
- Unit: Nm

### Principle of Moments
> For equilibrium: Sum of clockwise moments = Sum of anticlockwise moments

### Centre of Gravity
- Point where weight appears to act
- For regular shapes: geometric centre
- Object balances when pivot is below centre of gravity

### Stability
- **Stable:** Returns to position after small displacement (low CoG, wide base)
- **Unstable:** Falls over after small displacement
- **Neutral:** Stays in new position

## 5. GRAVITY

### Gravitational Field Strength (g)
- Force per unit mass
- g ≈ 10 N/kg on Earth
- g ≈ 1.6 N/kg on Moon

### Weight on Different Planets
```
W = mg
```
- Mass stays same
- Weight changes with g

## 6. ENERGY

### Forms of Energy
- Kinetic
- Gravitational potential
- Elastic potential
- Chemical
- Nuclear
- Thermal
- Electrical
- Light
- Sound

### Energy Transfers
- Mechanical working
- Electrical working
- Heating
- Radiation (waves)

### Kinetic Energy
```
KE = ½mv²
```

### Gravitational Potential Energy
```
GPE = mgh
```

### Conservation of Energy
> Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one form to another.

### Efficiency
```
Efficiency = (Useful energy output / Total energy input) × 100%
Efficiency = (Useful power output / Total power input) × 100%
```

### Power
```
Power = Work done / Time
P = E / t
```
- Unit: Watt (W) = Joule/second

## 7. PRESSURE

### Definition
```
Pressure = Force / Area
P = F / A
```
- Unit: Pascal (Pa) = N/m²

### Pressure in Liquids
```
P = hρg
```
- h = depth
- ρ = density
- g = gravitational field strength

### Hydraulic Systems
- Pressure transmitted equally throughout fluid
- Force multiplication: F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂
- Applications: Car brakes, hydraulic lifts

### Atmospheric Pressure
- ~100,000 Pa at sea level
- Decreases with altitude
- Measured with barometer

## 8. THERMAL PHYSICS

### Temperature Scales
- Celsius (°C)
- Kelvin (K): K = °C + 273

### Thermal Expansion
- Solids, liquids, gases expand when heated
- Applications: Thermometers, bridges with expansion gaps

### Specific Heat Capacity
```
E = mcΔT
Energy = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change
```
- Amount of energy to raise 1 kg by 1°C
- Water: 4200 J/kg°C

### Specific Latent Heat
```
E = mL
Energy = mass × specific latent heat
```

| Type | When | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| **Latent heat of fusion** | Melting/freezing | Ice ↔ Water |
| **Latent heat of vaporization** | Boiling/condensing | Water ↔ Steam |

### Conduction
- Heat transfer through solids
- Metals: Free electrons transfer energy quickly
- Non-metals: Slower, vibrating particles

### Convection
- Heat transfer in fluids (liquids + gases)
- Hot fluid rises, cool fluid sinks
- Creates convection currents

### Radiation
- Heat transfer by infrared waves
- No medium needed
- Dark, matte surfaces: Good absorbers/emitters
- Light, shiny surfaces: Poor absorbers/emitters

### Evaporation
- Liquid → gas at surface (below boiling point)
- Cooling effect (fastest molecules escape)
- Factors: Temperature, surface area, draught, humidity

## 9. WAVES

### Wave Terms

| Term | Symbol | Definition |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Wavelength** | λ | Distance between wave crests |
| **Frequency** | f | Waves per second (Hz) |
| **Amplitude** | A | Maximum displacement from rest |
| **Wave speed** | v | Speed of wave |
| **Period** | T | Time for one wave |

```
v = fλ
Wave speed = frequency × wavelength
T = 1/f
```

### Types of Waves

| | Transverse | Longitudinal |
|---|-----------|-------------|
| **Oscillation** | Perpendicular to direction | Parallel to direction |
| **Examples** | Light, water, radio | Sound, compression in spring |
| **Parts** | Crest, trough | Compression, rarefaction |

### Properties of Waves
- **Reflection:** Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection
- **Refraction:** Change in speed → change in direction
- **Diffraction:** Spread out after passing through gap
- **Interference:** Constructive (in phase) and destructive (out of phase)

### Electromagnetic Spectrum

| Type | Wavelength | Uses |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Radio** | Longest | Communication |
| **Microwave** | | Cooking, phones |
| **Infrared** | | Remote controls, thermal imaging |
| **Visible light** | | Vision, photography |
| **Ultraviolet** | | Sun tanning, security |
| **X-rays** | | Medical imaging |
| **Gamma rays** | Shortest | Cancer treatment, sterilization |

**All EM waves:**
- Transverse
- Travel at speed of light (3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum)
- Can travel through vacuum
- Obey v = fλ

### Sound
- Longitudinal wave
- Requires medium
- Speed: ~330 m/s in air
- Pitch: Frequency (higher = higher pitch)
- Loudness: Amplitude (larger = louder)
- Echo: Reflected sound

## 10. LIGHT

### Reflection
- Plane mirror: Virtual, upright, same size, laterally inverted
- Curved mirrors: Concave (converging), convex (diverging)

### Refraction
- Light bends when passing between media
- n = sin i / sin r (Snell's law)
- Speed changes, frequency stays same

### Critical Angle & Total Internal Reflection
- When light goes from denser to less dense medium
- Critical angle: Angle where refracted ray is along boundary
- Total internal reflection: Angle > critical angle
- Uses: Optical fibres, prisms in binoculars

### Lenses
- **Convex (converging):** Thicker in middle, focuses light
- **Concave (diverging):** Thinner in middle, spreads light
- Ray diagrams to find image position

### Dispersion
- White light splits into colours (spectrum)
- Red refracts least, violet most
- Rainbow, prism effect

## 11. MAGNETISM

### Properties of Magnets
- North and south poles
- Like poles repel, opposite poles attract
- Magnetic field: Region where force acts

### Magnetic Field Patterns
- Bar magnet: Lines from N to S
- Straight wire: Concentric circles
- Solenoid: Like bar magnet

### Electromagnetism
- Current creates magnetic field
- Right-hand grip rule for direction

### Electromagnets
- Magnetic only when current flows
- Strength factors: Current, number of coils, iron core
- Uses: Relays, cranes, MRI scanners

### Motor Effect
- Current-carrying wire in magnetic field experiences force
- Fleming's left-hand rule: Thumb (motion), First finger (field), seCond finger (current)
- DC motor: Coil rotates due to forces

### Electromagnetic Induction
- Moving magnet near coil induces voltage
- Factors: Speed, strength, number of turns
- AC generator: Rotating coil in magnetic field
- Transformer: Changes voltage using electromagnetic induction

## 12. ELECTRICITY

### Electric Charge
- Positive (+) and negative (-)
- Like charges repel, opposite charges attract
- SI unit: Coulomb (C)

### Current
```
I = Q / t
Current = Charge / Time
```
- Unit: Ampere (A)
- Conventional current: + to -
- Electron flow: - to +

### Voltage (Potential Difference)
```
V = W / Q
Voltage = Work done / Charge
```
- Unit: Volt (V)
- Energy transferred per unit charge

### Resistance
```
R = V / I
Resistance = Voltage / Current
```
- Unit: Ohm (Ω)

**Ohm's Law:**
```
V = IR
```
- For ohmic conductors at constant temperature

### Resistors in Series
```
R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...
```

### Resistors in Parallel
```
1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ...
```

### Electrical Power
```
P = IV
P = I²R
P = V²/R
```

### Electrical Energy
```
E = IVt
Energy = Current × Voltage × Time
```
- Unit: Joule (J) or kilowatt-hour (kWh)
- 1 kWh = 3,600,000 J

### Domestic Electricity
- Mains: 230V AC (UK)
- Three-pin plug: Live (brown), Neutral (blue), Earth (green/yellow)
- Fuse/circuit breaker: Safety device
- Double insulation: No earth needed

### Transformers
```
Vp/Vs = Np/Ns
```
- **Step-up:** More turns in secondary, increases voltage
- **Step-down:** Fewer turns in secondary, decreases voltage
- Works only with AC

## 13. ELECTRIC CIRCUITS

### Circuit Symbols
- Cell, battery, resistor, lamp, switch, ammeter, voltmeter

### Series Circuits
- Single pathway
- Same current throughout
- Voltage shared between components
- One break = whole circuit stops

### Parallel Circuits
- Multiple pathways
- Same voltage across each branch
- Current shared between branches
- One break = other branches work

### Measuring
- **Ammeter:** Series, measures current
- **Voltmeter:** Parallel, measures voltage

### Potential Dividers
- Two resistors in series
- Voltage divides in ratio of resistances
- Applications: Sensor circuits, volume controls

### LDR (Light Dependent Resistor)
- Light ↑ → Resistance ↓
- Used in automatic lights

### Thermistor
- Temperature ↑ → Resistance ↓
- Used in temperature sensors

## 14. ATOMIC PHYSICS

### Structure of Atom
- Nucleus: Protons (+) and neutrons (0)
- Electrons (-) orbit in shells
- Mostly empty space

### Radioactivity
- Spontaneous decay of unstable nuclei
- Three types of radiation:

| Type | Symbol | Charge | Mass | Penetration | Ionization |
|------|--------|--------|------|-------------|------------|
| **Alpha** | α | +2 | 4 | Stopped by paper | Strong |
| **Beta** | β⁻ | -1 | 1/1840 | Stopped by aluminium | Moderate |
| **Gamma** | γ | 0 | 0 | Reduced by lead | Weak |

### Half-Life
- Time for half of radioactive nuclei to decay
- Constant for given isotope
- Used in: Carbon dating, medical tracers

### Background Radiation
- Sources: Radon gas, cosmic rays, rocks, medical
- About 2-3 mSv per year average

### Nuclear Equations
- Mass number and atomic number conserved
- Alpha decay: Mass -4, atomic number -2
- Beta decay: Mass same, atomic number +1

### Nuclear Fission
- Heavy nucleus splits into two
- Releases energy + neutrons
- Chain reaction
- Used in nuclear power stations

### Nuclear Fusion
- Light nuclei join together
- Releases more energy than fission
- Requires high temperature/pressure
- Occurs in stars
- Difficult to control on Earth

## 15. SPACE PHYSICS

### Solar System
- Sun at centre
- Planets orbit in ellipses
- Order: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune

### Orbits
- Circular motion due to gravity
- Speed constant, direction changes
- Further planets: Longer orbital period, slower speed

### Satellites
- **Geostationary:** Orbit matches Earth's rotation (24 hours), stays above same point
- **Polar:** Orbit over poles, covers entire Earth

### The Universe
- Stars form from clouds of gas (nebulae)
- Fusion of hydrogen → helium provides energy
- Lifecycle: Main sequence → Red giant → White dwarf (or supernova → neutron star/black hole for massive stars)

### Big Bang Theory
- Universe began from extremely hot, dense point
- Evidence: Red shift of galaxies, cosmic microwave background radiation
- Universe is expanding

### Red Shift
- Light from distant galaxies shifted towards red (longer wavelength)
- Indicates galaxies moving away from us
- Further galaxies → faster recession → greater red shift

## EXAM TIPS

1. **Show all working** — Formula → Substitution → Answer with units
2. **Check units** — Convert to standard units (m, kg, s, A)
3. **Significant figures** — Match data given (usually 2 or 3 sf)
4. **Rearrange formulas** — Practice making different variables the subject
5. **Read graphs carefully** — Note scales, labels, whether straight or curved
6. **Define terms precisely** — Use correct physics vocabulary
7. **Diagrams:** Draw large, label clearly, use ruler
8. **Circuit problems:** Trace current paths, remember series/parallel rules
9. **Wave calculations:** Watch units for wavelength (m, cm, nm)
10. **Nuclear equations:** Check mass and atomic numbers balance
