## 1. ATOMIC STRUCTURE

### Fundamental Particles

| Particle | Relative Mass | Relative Charge | Location |
|----------|--------------|-----------------|----------|
| **Proton** | 1 | +1 | Nucleus |
| **Neutron** | 1 | 0 | Nucleus |
| **Electron** | 1/1836 | –1 | Shells/orbitals |

> **Atomic number (Z):** Number of protons (defines the element)
> **Mass number (A):** Protons + neutrons
> **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons

### Calculations
```
Number of neutrons = A – Z
Number of electrons = Z – charge (cation: –, anion: +)
```

### Mass Spectrometry
**Five stages:**
1. **Ionisation** — Electron gun knocks off an electron → M⁺ (molecular ion)
2. **Acceleration** — Positive ions accelerated by electric field
3. **Deflection** — Magnetic field deflects ions by mass/charge ratio (m/z)
4. **Detection** — Ion hits detector; abundance measured
5. **Output** — Mass spectrum produced

**Relative Atomic Mass (Ar):**
```
Ar = Σ (isotopic mass × relative abundance) / Σ relative abundance
```

### Electronic Configuration
**Rules:** Aufbau principle, Pauli exclusion principle, Hund's rule

| Subshell | s | p | d | f |
|----------|---|---|---|---|
| **Max electrons** | 2 | 6 | 10 | 14 |

**Order of filling:** 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p → 5s → 4d → 5p → 6s → 4f → 5d → 6p...

**Examples:**
- Na (11): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹
- Fe (26): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁶ (or [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s²)
- Cr (24): [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ (exception — half-filled d-subshell is more stable)
- Cu (29): [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (exception — full d-subshell is more stable)

> **Exam tip:** For transition metals, remove 4s electrons BEFORE 3d when forming cations (e.g., Fe²⁺ = [Ar] 3d⁶)

## 2. CHEMICAL BONDING

### Ionic Bonding
- Electrostatic attraction between **oppositely charged ions**
- Metal → loses electrons → **cation** (+)
- Non-metal → gains electrons → **anion** (–)
- **Giant ionic lattice** structure
- High melting/boiling points; conduct when molten/aqueous
- Soluble in water; brittle

### Covalent Bonding
- **Shared pair** of electrons between atoms
- Can be single (σ), double (σ + π), or triple (σ + 2π)
- **Diatomic molecules:** H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂

| Property | Ionic | Covalent (Simple) | Covalent (Giant) |
|----------|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Structure** | Giant lattice | Small molecules | Giant network |
| **Examples** | NaCl, MgO | CO₂, H₂O | Diamond, SiO₂ |
| **Melting point** | High | Low | Very high |
| **Conductivity** | Molten/aqueous only | None | Variable |
| **Solubility (water)** | Often soluble | Often insoluble | Insoluble |

### Metallic Bonding
- **Delocalised electrons** in a lattice of positive metal ions
- Properties: malleable, ductile, good conductors, lustrous
- Strength increases with: more delocalised electrons, smaller ionic radius, higher charge

### Intermolecular Forces (IMFs)

| Force | Relative Strength | Occurs Between | Example |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|---------|
| **London dispersion forces (LDF)** | Weakest | All molecules | Noble gases, alkanes |
| **Dipole-dipole** | Weak | Polar molecules | HCl, aldehydes |
| **Hydrogen bonding** | Strongest IMF | H bonded to N, O, or F | H₂O, NH₃, alcohols |

> **Hydrogen bonding** explains: high bp of water, ice density < water, surface tension, DNA base pairing

### Bond Polarity & Electronegativity
- **Electronegativity:** Measure of an atom's ability to attract bonding electrons
- **F > O > Cl > N > Br > C > H** (decreasing)
- **Polar bond:** ΔEN ≈ 0.4–1.7 → dipole
- **Ionic bond:** ΔEN > 1.7

### Shapes of Molecules (VSEPR Theory)

| Electron pairs | Bonding pairs | Lone pairs | Shape | Bond angle | Example |
|----------------|---------------|------------|-------|------------|---------|
| 2 | 2 | 0 | **Linear** | 180° | CO₂, BeCl₂ |
| 3 | 3 | 0 | **Trigonal planar** | 120° | BF₃ |
| 4 | 4 | 0 | **Tetrahedral** | 109.5° | CH₄ |
| 4 | 3 | 1 | **Trigonal pyramidal** | ~107° | NH₃ |
| 4 | 2 | 2 | **Bent** | ~104.5° | H₂O |
| 5 | 5 | 0 | **Trigonal bipyramidal** | 90°, 120° | PCl₅ |
| 6 | 6 | 0 | **Octahedral** | 90° | SF₆ |

> **Lone pair repulsion > bonding pair repulsion** → bond angles decrease with lone pairs

## 3. STATES OF MATTER

### Kinetic Theory
- Particles in **constant random motion**
- Particles have **negligible volume** compared to container
- **No intermolecular forces** in ideal gases
- Collisions are perfectly **elastic**

### Ideal Gas Equation
```
PV = nRT
```
- **P** = pressure (Pa)
- **V** = volume (m³)
- **n** = moles (mol)
- **R** = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
- **T** = temperature (K) = °C + 273

**Combined gas law (fixed n):**
```
P₁V₁ / T₁ = P₂V₂ / T₂
```

**Molar volume at RTP (25°C, 1 atm):** 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹
**Molar volume at STP (0°C, 1 atm):** 22.4 dm³ mol⁻¹

### Real Gases vs Ideal Gases
| Ideal | Real |
|-------|------|
| No IMF | Have IMF |
| Negligible particle volume | Particles have volume |
| Perfectly elastic collisions | Collisions slightly inelastic |

> Real gases behave most ideally at **high temperature** and **low pressure**

### Solids, Liquids, Gases

| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|----------|-------|--------|-----|
| **Particle arrangement** | Regular, fixed | Close, random | Far apart, random |
| **Particle motion** | Vibrate in fixed positions | Slide past each other | Rapid, free movement |
| **Shape** | Fixed | Takes shape of container | Fills container |
| **Volume** | Fixed | Fixed | Variable |
| **Compressibility** | Incompressible | Almost incompressible | Compressible |

## 4. CHEMICAL ENERGETICS

### Enthalpy Changes (ΔH)
> **Enthalpy change:** Heat energy change at constant pressure

| Type | Definition | Sign |
|------|-----------|------|
| **Exothermic** | Heat released to surroundings | ΔH < 0 (negative) |
| **Endothermic** | Heat absorbed from surroundings | ΔH > 0 (positive) |

**Standard conditions:** 100 kPa (1 bar), 298 K (25°C), 1 mol dm⁻³ for solutions, stated state

### Key Enthalpy Definitions

| Term | Symbol | Definition |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Standard enthalpy of formation** | ΔH°f | Enthalpy change when 1 mole of compound forms from its elements in standard states |
| **Standard enthalpy of combustion** | ΔH°c | Enthalpy change when 1 mole of substance burns completely in oxygen |
| **Standard enthalpy of neutralisation** | ΔH°neut | Enthalpy change when 1 mole of water forms from acid + base |
| **Standard enthalpy of atomisation** | ΔH°at | Enthalpy change when 1 mole of gaseous atoms forms from element |
| **Lattice enthalpy** | ΔH°latt | Enthalpy change when 1 mole of ionic solid forms from gaseous ions |
| **Enthalpy of hydration** | ΔH°hyd | Enthalpy change when 1 mole of gaseous ions dissolves in water |
| **Enthalpy of solution** | ΔH°sol | Enthalpy change when 1 mole of solute dissolves in water |

### Hess's Law
> **The enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the route taken.**

**Cycle method:**
```
ΔH_reaction = Σ ΔH°f (products) – Σ ΔH°f (reactants)
ΔH_reaction = Σ ΔH°c (reactants) – Σ ΔH°c (products)
```

### Bond Enthalpies
```
ΔH = Σ (bonds broken) – Σ (bonds formed)
```
- Bond breaking = **endothermic** (+)
- Bond forming = **exothermic** (–)
- **Average bond enthalpies** are used for estimates (from many compounds)
- **Exact bond dissociation enthalpies** are specific to one compound

### Born-Haber Cycles
> Constructed to calculate **lattice enthalpy** indirectly

**For NaCl(s):**
```
Na(s) + ½Cl₂(g) → NaCl(s)     ΔH°f
```
**Steps (atomisation, ionisation, electron affinity):**
1. Na(s) → Na(g) — atomisation (+)
2. Na(g) → Na⁺(g) + e⁻ — 1st ionisation energy (+)
3. ½Cl₂(g) → Cl(g) — atomisation (+)
4. Cl(g) + e⁻ → Cl⁻(g) — electron affinity (–)
5. Na⁺(g) + Cl⁻(g) → NaCl(s) — lattice enthalpy (–)

**Formula:**
```
ΔH°f = ΔH°at(Na) + IE₁(Na) + ΔH°at(Cl) + EA(Cl) + ΔH°latt
```

> **Exam tip:** Learn the cycle direction — atomise, ionise, add electrons, then lattice formation (always exothermic)

## 5. ELECTROCHEMISTRY

### Redox Reactions
- **Oxidation:** Loss of electrons, increase in oxidation number
- **Reduction:** Gain of electrons, decrease in oxidation number

**Mnemonic: OIL RIG**
- **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss
- **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain

### Rules for Oxidation Numbers
1. Uncombined element = 0
2. Monatomic ion = charge on ion
3. Oxygen = –2 (except peroxides = –1, OF₂ = +2)
4. Hydrogen = +1 (except metal hydrides = –1)
5. Group 1 = +1, Group 2 = +2
6. Sum in neutral compound = 0; in ion = charge on ion

### Standard Electrode Potentials (E°)
> Measured against the **standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)** — Pt | H₂(g) | H⁺(aq) at 298 K, 100 kPa, 1 mol dm⁻³

**Cell potential:**
```
E°cell = E°(reduction, cathode) – E°(oxidation, anode)
E°cell = E°(right) – E°(left)   [for conventional cell diagram]
```

**Cell diagram:**
```
Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq) || Cu²⁺(aq) | Cu(s)
```
- Left = oxidation (anode)
- Right = reduction (cathode)
- Single line = phase boundary
- Double line = salt bridge

**Predicting spontaneity:**
- E°cell > 0 → reaction spontaneous under standard conditions
- More positive E° = stronger oxidising agent (undergoes reduction)
- More negative E° = stronger reducing agent (undergoes oxidation)

### Electrolysis
- **Electrolytic cell:** Converts electrical energy → chemical energy
- **Anode:** Positive electrode — oxidation occurs
- **Cathode:** Negative electrode — reduction occurs

**Rules for products:**
| Electrolyte | Cathode product | Anode product |
|-------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Molten ionic compound | Metal | Non-metal |
| Aqueous salt (concentrated) | Metal (if less reactive than H) | Halogen |
| Aqueous salt (dilute) | H₂ (from water) | O₂ (from water) |
| Aqueous NaCl | H₂ | Cl₂ (conc.) / O₂ (dilute) |

**Quantitative electrolysis (Faraday's laws):**
```
Q = I × t          (charge = current × time)
moles of electrons = Q / F    where F = 96500 C mol⁻¹
```

## 6. EQUILIBRIA

### Dynamic Equilibrium
> **Forward rate = Reverse rate**; concentrations of reactants and products remain constant (but not necessarily equal)

### Equilibrium Constant Kc
For: aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
```
Kc = [C]^c [D]^d / [A]^a [B]^b
```
- Concentrations at equilibrium
- Only temperature changes Kc
- Pure solids and liquids omitted

### Equilibrium Constant Kp (gases)
```
Kp = (P_C)^c (P_D)^d / (P_A)^a (P_B)^b
```
- Partial pressures used
- Partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure

**Relationship:** Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn  where Δn = moles gaseous products – moles gaseous reactants

### Le Chatelier's Principle
> **If a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed, the system adjusts to minimise the disturbance.**

| Change | Effect on equilibrium | Kc/Kp |
|--------|----------------------|-------|
| **↑ Concentration** | Shifts away from added substance | Unchanged |
| **↑ Pressure** | Shifts to side with fewer gas moles | Unchanged |
| **↑ Temperature** | Shifts in endothermic direction | Changes |
| **Catalyst** | No shift; speeds up both rates equally | Unchanged |

### Haber Process (Ammonia)
```
N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)    ΔH = –92 kJ mol⁻¹
```
| Condition | Choice | Reason |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Temperature | ~450°C | Compromise between rate and yield (exothermic) |
| Pressure | ~200 atm | High pressure favours products (fewer moles) |
| Catalyst | Iron | Increases rate; no effect on yield |
| Remove NH₃ | Continuous | Keeps equilibrium shifting right |

### Contact Process (Sulfuric Acid)
```
2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)    ΔH = –98 kJ mol⁻¹
```
- Temperature: ~450°C (compromise)
- Pressure: 1–2 atm (acceptable yield at low pressure)
- Catalyst: Vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅)

## 7. REACTION KINETICS

### Rate of Reaction
```
Rate = change in concentration / time     (mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹)
```
- Found from **gradients** of concentration-time graphs
- Initial rate = gradient at t = 0

### Rate Equations
```
Rate = k[A]^m [B]^n
```
- **k** = rate constant (temperature-dependent)
- **m, n** = orders of reaction with respect to A and B
- **Overall order** = m + n

| Order | Rate ∝ | Half-life |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| **Zero** | [A]⁰ = constant | t½ decreases as [A] decreases |
| **First** | [A]¹ | t½ is constant |
| **Second** | [A]² | t½ increases as [A] decreases |

### Determining Orders
- **Initial rates method:** Compare initial rates at different initial concentrations
- **Graphical method:**
  - Plot [A] vs t → straight line = zero order
  - Plot ln[A] vs t → straight line = first order
  - Plot 1/[A] vs t → straight line = second order

### Arrhenius Equation
```
k = A × e^(–Ea / RT)
ln k = ln A – Ea / RT
```
- **A** = pre-exponential factor (frequency factor)
- **Ea** = activation energy (J mol⁻¹)
- **R** = 8.314 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹
- **T** = temperature (K)

**Plot of ln k vs 1/T gives straight line:**
- Gradient = –Ea / R
- Intercept = ln A

### Catalysis
- **Homogeneous catalyst:** Same phase as reactants
- **Heterogeneous catalyst:** Different phase (usually solid with gas/liquid reactants)
- Provides alternative reaction pathway with **lower Ea**
- Not consumed in reaction
- May be poisoned by impurities

## 8. INORGANIC CHEMISTRY

### Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals)

| Property | Trend down group | Reason |
|----------|------------------|--------|
| Atomic radius | Increases | More electron shells |
| 1st ionisation energy | Decreases | Outer electron further from nucleus, more shielding |
| Electronegativity | Decreases | Less attraction for bonding electrons |
| Reactivity | Increases | Easier to lose electrons |
| Melting point | Decreases | Metallic bonding weaker (larger ions, delocalised electrons further apart) |

**Reactions:**
- With water: M + 2H₂O → M(OH)₂ + H₂ (reactivity: Mg << Ca < Sr < Ba)
- With oxygen: 2M + O₂ → 2MO
- With dilute acid: M + 2HCl → MCl₂ + H₂

**Hydroxide solubility:** Increases down group (Ba(OH)₂ most soluble)
**Sulfate solubility:** Decreases down group (BaSO₄ insoluble — **test for sulfate**)

### Group 17 (Halogens)

| Property | Trend down group | Reason |
|----------|------------------|--------|
| Atomic radius | Increases | More shells |
| Electronegativity | Decreases | Bonding electrons further from nucleus |
| Melting/boiling point | Increases | More electrons → stronger LDF |
| Reactivity | Decreases | Harder to gain electron |
| Colour intensity | Darker | F₂ (pale yellow) → Cl₂ (green) → Br₂ (red-brown) → I₂ (purple-black) |

**Displacement reactions:**
- More reactive halogen displaces less reactive halide from solution
- Cl₂ + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br₂
- Br₂ + 2KI → 2KBr + I₂

**Tests for halides:** Add dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq)
| Halide | Precipitate colour | Solubility in NH₃(aq) |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Cl⁻ | White | Soluble in dilute NH₃ |
| Br⁻ | Cream | Soluble only in conc. NH₃ |
| I⁻ | Yellow | Insoluble in NH₃ |

### Transition Metals
**Characteristics:**
- Partially filled **d-subshell**
- Variable oxidation states (e.g., Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺, Cu⁺/Cu²⁺, Mn²⁺/MnO₄⁻)
- Form **coloured compounds**
- Form **complex ions**
- Act as **catalysts**

### Complex Ions
- **Ligand:** Molecule/ion with lone pair that donates to metal ion
- **Coordination number:** Number of dative bonds to metal (commonly 4 or 6)
- **Monodentate:** One donor atom (H₂O, NH₃, Cl⁻, CN⁻)
- **Bidentate:** Two donor atoms (en = ethane-1,2-diamine, oxalate)
- **Hexadentate:** Six donor atoms (EDTA⁴⁻)

**Shapes:**
- 6-coordinate → **octahedral** (e.g., [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺)
- 4-coordinate → **tetrahedral** (e.g., [CuCl₄]²⁻) or **square planar**

**Ligand substitution:**
```
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O
```

### Colours of Transition Metal Complexes
| Complex | Colour |
|---------|--------|
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | Pale blue |
| [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | Deep blue |
| [CuCl₄]²⁻ | Yellow-green |
| [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | Pink |
| [CoCl₄]²⁻ | Blue |
| [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | Pale green |
| [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ | Yellow/brown |
| [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ | Ruby/violet |
| MnO₄⁻ | Purple |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ | Orange |
| CrO₄²⁻ | Yellow |

> **Exam tip:** Learn these colours — they appear in qualitative analysis and redox titrations

## 9. ORGANIC CHEMISTRY — INTRODUCTION

### Homologous Series
> A family of compounds with the **same functional group**, each differing by **CH₂**

| Series | Functional Group | General Formula | Suffix |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Alkane** | None (C–C only) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | -ane |
| **Alkene** | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ | -ene |
| **Alcohol** | –OH | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | -ol |
| **Halogenoalkane** | –X (X = F, Cl, Br, I) | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X | prefix: fluoro/chloro/bromo/iodo |
| **Aldehyde** | –CHO | CₙH₂ₙO | -al |
| **Ketone** | C=O | CₙH₂ₙO | -one |
| **Carboxylic acid** | –COOH | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | -oic acid |
| **Ester** | –COO– | — | -yl -oate |
| **Amine** | –NH₂ | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NH₂ | -amine |
| **Amide** | –CONH₂ | — | -amide |

### IUPAC Nomenclature Rules
1. Find the **longest carbon chain** containing the principal functional group
2. Number from the end nearest to the principal functional group (lowest locant)
3. Name substituents alphabetically with locants
4. Principal functional group determines suffix; others become prefixes

### Types of Isomerism

#### Structural Isomerism
| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Chain** | Different carbon skeleton | Butane vs methylpropane |
| **Position** | Same skeleton, different position of functional group | Propan-1-ol vs propan-2-ol |
| **Functional group** | Same formula, different functional group | Propanal vs propanone |

#### Stereoisomerism
| Type | Description | Requirements |
|------|-------------|--------------|
| **E/Z (geometric)** | Different arrangement around C=C | Each C of double bond has two different groups |
| **Optical** | Non-superimposable mirror images | Chiral centre (C with 4 different groups) |

> **E isomer:** Higher priority groups on **opposite** sides of C=C
> **Z isomer:** Higher priority groups on **same** side

**Cahn-Ingold-Prelog priority rules:** Higher atomic number = higher priority

### Organic Reaction Mechanisms
**Curly arrows:** Show movement of electron pairs
- Full arrowhead = movement of **electron pair**
- Half arrowhead = movement of **single electron** (radicals)

**Types of reagents:**
| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| **Electrophile** | Electron pair acceptor | H⁺, Br₂, NO₂⁺ |
| **Nucleophile** | Electron pair donor | OH⁻, NH₃, H₂O, CN⁻ |
| **Free radical** | Species with unpaired electron | Cl•, CH₃• |

## 10. ALKANES & ALKENES

### Alkanes (Saturated Hydrocarbons)
**General formula:** CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

**Reactions:**
1. **Combustion:**
   - Complete: C₂H₆ + 3.5O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O
   - Incomplete: produces CO or C (soot)

2. **Free radical substitution (with halogens):**
   ```
   Initiation: Cl₂ → 2Cl•
   Propagation: CH₄ + Cl• → •CH₃ + HCl
                •CH₃ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•
   Termination: Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂
                Cl• + •CH₃ → CH₃Cl
   ```
   - Requires **UV light**
   - Produces mixture of products (CH₃Cl, CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃, CCl₄)
   - Further substitution occurs because product is also reactive

### Alkenes (Unsaturated)
**General formula:** CₙH₂ₙ
- **Planar** around C=C; bond angle ~120°

**Reactions:**
1. **Electrophilic addition of H₂ (hydrogenation):**
   - Conditions: Ni/Pt/Pd catalyst, 150°C
   - CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃CH₃

2. **Electrophilic addition of halogens:**
   - CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br (1,2-dibromoethane)
   - **Test for unsaturation:** Br₂(aq) or Br₂ in CCl₄ decolourises

3. **Electrophilic addition of hydrogen halides:**
   - Markovnikov's rule: H adds to C with more H atoms
   - CH₃CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CHBrCH₃ (major)

4. **Electrophilic addition of steam (hydration):**
   - Conditions: H₃PO₄ catalyst, 300°C, 60 atm
   - CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH

5. **Oxidation by cold dilute acidified KMnO₄:**
   - Purple → colourless (diol formed)
   - CH₂=CH₂ + [O] + H₂O → CH₂OHCH₂OH

6. **Oxidation by hot concentrated acidified KMnO₄:**
   - Cleaves C=C bond
   - CH₃CH=CH₂ → CH₃COOH + CO₂ + H₂O

### Polymerisation
**Addition polymerisation:**
- Monomers add together without losing any atoms
- Requires C=C bond
- **Poly(ethene), poly(chloroethene) [PVC], poly(tetrafluoroethene) [PTFE], poly(propene)**

**Repeating unit:** Remove double bond, extend bonds at each end, place in brackets with subscript n

**Disposal issues:**
- Non-biodegradable → landfill/incineration problems
- **Recycling** or development of **biodegradable polymers** as solutions

## 11. ALCOHOLS, HALOGENOALKANES & CARBONYLS

### Alcohols (–OH)
**Classification:**
| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Primary (1°)** | –CH₂OH | Ethanol |
| **Secondary (2°)** | –CHOH– | Propan-2-ol |
| **Tertiary (3°)** | –COH– (no H on C) | 2-methylpropan-2-ol |

**Reactions of Alcohols:**
1. **Combustion:** Complete → CO₂ + H₂O

2. **Substitution with HX:**
   - C₂H₅OH + HBr → C₂H₅Br + H₂O
   - Reactivity: HI > HBr > HCl

3. **Elimination (dehydration) to alkene:**
   - Conditions: Conc. H₂SO₄ or Al₂O₃ catalyst, heat
   - C₂H₅OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
   - For 2°/3° alcohols: Zaitsev's rule — more substituted alkene is major product

4. **Oxidation:**
   | Alcohol type | Oxidising agent | Product |
   |--------------|-----------------|---------|
   | Primary | Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange → green) | Aldehyde (distil) → Carboxylic acid (reflux) |
   | Secondary | Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | Ketone (not further oxidised) |
   | Tertiary | Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | No reaction |

### Halogenoalkanes
**Nucleophilic substitution (SN1/SN2):**
```
SN2: CH₃CH₂Br + OH⁻ → CH₃CH₂OH + Br⁻
     (primary, one step, inversion of configuration)

SN1: (CH₃)₃CBr + OH⁻ → (CH₃)₃COH + Br⁻
     (tertiary, two steps, carbocation intermediate, racemic mixture if chiral)
```

**Rate:** Tertiary > Secondary > Primary (for SN1)
**Rate:** Primary > Secondary > Tertiary (for SN2)

**Factors affecting rate:**
- **C–X bond strength:** C–I > C–Br > C–Cl (weaker bond = faster)
- **Nucleophile strength:** OH⁻ > H₂O
- **Polarity of solvent:** Polar protic favours SN1; polar aprotic favours SN2

**Elimination (to alkene):**
- Conditions: Ethanolic KOH, heat
- Competes with substitution; high temperature favours elimination

### Carbonyl Compounds

| Property | Aldehyde | Ketone |
|----------|----------|--------|
| Structure | R–CHO | R–CO–R' |
| Oxidation | Oxidised to carboxylic acid | Resistant |
| Tollens' reagent | Silver mirror formed | No reaction |
| Fehling's/Benedict's | Brick-red precipitate | No reaction |

**Nucleophilic addition reactions:**
1. **With HCN (cyanohydrin formation):**
   - CH₃CHO + HCN → CH₃CH(OH)CN
   - Increases carbon chain by 1
   - Product can be hydrolysed to 2-hydroxy acid or amino acid

2. **With NaBH₄ (reduction):**
   - CH₃CHO + [H] → CH₃CH₂OH (primary alcohol)
   - CH₃COCH₃ + [H] → CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ (secondary alcohol)

> **Tollens' test:** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + aldehyde → Ag (silver mirror) + carboxylate
> **Fehling's test:** Cu²⁺ (blue) + aldehyde → Cu₂O (brick-red) + carboxylate

## 12. CARBOXYLIC ACIDS, ESTERS & NITROGEN COMPOUNDS

### Carboxylic Acids (–COOH)
**Properties:**
- Weak acids: partially dissociate in water
- CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺
- React with carbonates, metals, bases

**Reactions:**
1. **Neutralisation:** CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O
2. **With metal:** 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂
3. **With carbonate:** 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂

### Esters
**Formation (esterification):**
```
Carboxylic acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester + Water
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
```
- Conditions: Conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat, reflux
- **Reversible** — use excess reactant or remove product to increase yield

**Hydrolysis:**
- **Acid hydrolysis:** Reverse of esterification
- **Alkaline hydrolysis (saponification):**
  ```
  CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH
  ```
  - Produces carboxylate salt + alcohol
  - Used in soap making

**Uses:** Solvents, flavourings, perfumes, plastics (polyesters)

### Amines
**Classification:**
| Type | Structure | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Primary (1°) | RNH₂ | Methylamine |
| Secondary (2°) | R₂NH | Dimethylamine |
| Tertiary (3°) | R₃N | Trimethylamine |

**Properties:**
- Weak bases (lone pair on N accepts H⁺)
- Basicity: Aliphatic > Aromatic (lone pair delocalised into benzene ring in arylamines)
- React with acids: RNH₂ + HCl → RNH₃⁺Cl⁻

**Preparation:**
- Reduction of nitriles: RCN + 4[H] → RCH₂NH₂ (LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni)
- Reduction of nitro compounds: C₆H₅NO₂ + 6[H] → C₆H₅NH₂ + 2H₂O (Sn/HCl or Fe/HCl)

### Amides
**Formation:**
```
Acyl chloride + amine → amide + HCl
CH₃COCl + NH₃ → CH₃CONH₂ + HCl
```

**Hydrolysis:**
- Acid: RCONH₂ + H₂O + H⁺ → RCOOH + NH₄⁺
- Alkaline: RCONH₂ + OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + NH₃

### Amino Acids
- Contain both **–NH₂** (basic) and **–COOH** (acidic)
- **Zwitterion** at intermediate pH: NH₃⁺–CHR–COO⁻ (dipolar, no net charge)
- **Isoelectric point:** pH where amino acid exists as zwitterion

**Reactions:**
1. With acid: NH₂–CHR–COOH + H⁺ → NH₃⁺–CHR–COOH
2. With base: NH₂–CHR–COOH + OH⁻ → NH₂–CHR–COO⁻ + H₂O

### Proteins & Polypeptides
- **Peptide bond (amide link):** –CO–NH– formed by condensation between amino acids
- **Primary structure:** Sequence of amino acids
- **Secondary structure:** α-helix, β-pleated sheet (hydrogen bonding)
- **Tertiary structure:** 3D folding (disulfide bridges, hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, LDF)
- **Quaternary structure:** Multiple polypeptide chains

**Hydrolysis of proteins:** Breaks peptide bonds → constituent amino acids (acid/alkaline/enzyme)

## 13. ORGANIC SYNTHESIS

### Retrosynthesis
> Working **backwards** from target molecule to available starting materials

**Key disconnections:**
| Target | Precursor | Reagent/Condition |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Alcohol | Alkene | H₂O/H⁺ or dilute acid |
| Alcohol | Halogenoalkane | Aqueous NaOH, reflux |
| Alkene | Alcohol | Conc. H₂SO₄ or Al₂O₃, heat |
| Halogenoalkane | Alcohol | Conc. HX or PCl₅/SOCl₂ |
| Carboxylic acid | Primary alcohol | Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, reflux |
| Aldehyde | Primary alcohol | Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, distil |
| Ketone | Secondary alcohol | Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, reflux |
| Nitrile | Halogenoalkane | KCN in ethanol, reflux |
| Amine | Nitrile | LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni |
| Ester | Carboxylic acid + Alcohol | Conc. H₂SO₄, heat |

### Functional Group Interconversions
```
Alkane → Halogenoalkane (free radical substitution, UV)
Halogenoalkane → Alcohol (NaOH(aq), reflux)
Alcohol → Alkene (conc. H₂SO₄ or Al₂O₃, heat)
Alkene → Alcohol (H₂O/H⁺ or H₂O/OH⁻)
Alkene → Halogenoalkane (HX)
Alcohol → Aldehyde → Carboxylic acid (oxidation)
Carboxylic acid → Ester (alcohol + acid catalyst)
Ester → Carboxylic acid + Alcohol (hydrolysis)
Halogenoalkane → Nitrile (KCN, ethanol)
Nitrile → Amine (reduction)
Nitrile → Carboxylic acid (acid/alkaline hydrolysis)
```

### Common Reagents Summary

| Reagent | What it does | Typical use |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **NaOH(aq), reflux** | Hydrolysis, substitution | Alcohol from halogenoalkane |
| **KOH(ethanol), reflux** | Elimination | Alkene from halogenoalkane |
| **Conc. H₂SO₄, heat** | Dehydration, esterification | Alkene from alcohol; ester |
| **K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, reflux** | Oxidation | Carboxylic acid/ketone |
| **K₂Cr₂O₇/H⁺, distil** | Mild oxidation | Aldehyde from 1° alcohol |
| **NaBH₄** | Reduction | Alcohol from carbonyl |
| **LiAlH₄** | Strong reduction | Amine from nitrile/nitro |
| **H₂/Ni (or Pd/Pt)** | Reduction | Alkane from alkene; amine from nitrile |
| **KCN, ethanol** | Nucleophilic substitution | Nitrile from halogenoalkane |
| **NH₃, ethanol, heat** | Nucleophilic substitution | Amine from halogenoalkane |
| **PCl₅ / SOCl₂** | Substitution | Halogenoalkane from alcohol |
| **Br₂(aq)** | Electrophilic addition | Test for C=C |
| **Tollens' reagent** | Oxidation | Test for aldehyde |
| **Fehling's/Benedict's** | Oxidation | Test for aldehyde |

## 14. ANALYTICAL TECHNIQUES

### Infrared (IR) Spectroscopy
- Measures absorption of IR radiation by molecular vibrations
- **Wavenumber (cm⁻¹)** = 1/λ

| Bond | Wavenumber (cm⁻¹) | Appearance |
|------|-------------------|------------|
| **O–H (alcohol)** | 3230–3550 | Broad, strong |
| **O–H (carboxylic acid)** | 2500–3300 | Very broad |
| **N–H** | 3300–3500 | Sharp |
| **C–H** | 2850–3100 | Sharp |
| **C≡N (nitrile)** | 2210–2260 | Sharp |
| **C=O** | 1680–1750 | Strong, sharp |
| **C=C** | 1620–1680 | Variable |
| **C–O** | 1000–1300 | Strong |

> **Fingerprint region:** 1500–500 cm⁻¹ — unique to each compound, used for matching against databases

### Mass Spectrometry (Organic)
**Molecular ion (M⁺):** Peak with highest m/z (excluding isotope peaks) → gives **molecular mass**

**Fragmentation patterns:**
- Cleavage at weak bonds or stable fragments
- **Base peak:** Most abundant fragment (set to 100% relative abundance)
- Common fragments: CH₃⁺ (m/z 15), C₂H₅⁺ (29), CO⁺ (28), CHO⁺ (29)

### Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) Spectroscopy

#### ¹H NMR (Proton NMR)
**Chemical shift (δ, ppm):**
| Proton environment | δ (ppm) |
|--------------------|---------|
| R–CH₃ | 0.9–1.0 |
| R–CH₂–R | 1.2–1.4 |
| R₃CH | 1.4–1.6 |
| C–C–Cl/Br | 3.0–4.0 |
| C=C–H | 4.5–6.5 |
| Ar–H | 6.5–8.5 |
| –CHO | 9.0–10.0 |
| –COOH | 10.0–12.0 |
| –OH, –NH | 1.0–5.0 (variable) |

**Integration trace:** Area under peak ∝ number of protons in that environment

**Splitting (n+1 rule):**
| Number of equivalent adjacent protons (n) | Splitting pattern |
|------------------------------------------|-------------------|
| 0 | Singlet (s) |
| 1 | Doublet (d) |
| 2 | Triplet (t) |
| 3 | Quartet (q) |
| 4 | Quintet |

> **n+1 rule:** A proton signal is split into n+1 peaks by n equivalent adjacent protons

**D₂O shake:** –OH and –NH protons exchange with D₂O and disappear from spectrum

#### ¹³C NMR (Carbon NMR)
- Each unique carbon environment gives one signal
- No splitting (rare isotope, low probability of two adjacent ¹³C)
- Chemical shifts:
  - Alkyl C: 0–50 ppm
  - C–O/N: 50–90 ppm
  - C=C/Ar: 100–160 ppm
  - C=O: 160–220 ppm

### Chromatography
**Principle:** Separation based on differential partitioning between stationary and mobile phases

| Type | Stationary phase | Mobile phase | Basis of separation |
|------|-----------------|--------------|---------------------|
| **TLC** | Silica/alumina (polar) | Solvent | Polarity |
| **Paper** | Water in paper | Solvent | Partitioning |
| **Gas (GC)** | High bp liquid on solid | Inert gas (N₂, He) | Boiling point/polarity |
| **HPLC** | Solid/liquid | Liquid under pressure | Polarity/size |

**Rf value (TLC):**
```
Rf = distance travelled by spot / distance travelled by solvent front
```
- Characteristic for a compound under given conditions
- Rf always between 0 and 1

**Retention time (GC/HPLC):** Time taken for compound to pass through column; used for identification

## 15. PERIODICITY

### Periodic Trends Across a Period (left to right)

| Property | Trend | Reason |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Atomic radius | Decreases | Increasing nuclear charge, same shielding |
| Ionic radius | Decreases (for same charge) | Increasing nuclear charge |
| Ionisation energy | Generally increases | Stronger attraction for electrons |
| Electronegativity | Increases | Greater attraction for bonding electrons |
| Metallic character | Decreases | Less tendency to lose electrons |
| Melting point | Increases then decreases | Giant metallic/network → simple molecular |

### Periodic Trends Down a Group

| Property | Trend | Reason |
|----------|-------|--------|
| Atomic radius | Increases | More electron shells |
| Ionisation energy | Decreases | Outer electron further from nucleus, more shielding |
| Electronegativity | Decreases | Weaker attraction for bonding electrons |
| Reactivity (metals) | Increases | Easier to lose electrons |
| Reactivity (non-metals) | Decreases | Harder to gain electrons |

### Oxides of Period 3
| Element | Oxide | Type | Reaction with water | pH of solution |
|---------|-------|------|---------------------|----------------|
| Na | Na₂O | Basic | Forms NaOH | 14 |
| Mg | MgO | Basic | Slightly soluble, forms Mg(OH)₂ | 9 |
| Al | Al₂O₃ | **Amphoteric** | Insoluble | — |
| Si | SiO₂ | Acidic | Insoluble | — |
| P | P₄O₁₀ | Acidic | Forms H₃PO₄ | 1–2 |
| S | SO₂/SO₃ | Acidic | Forms H₂SO₃/H₂SO₄ | 1 |
| Cl | Cl₂O₇ | Acidic | Forms HClO₄ | 1 |

**Amphoteric behaviour of Al₂O₃:**
- With acid: Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O
- With base: Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH + 3H₂O → 2Na[Al(OH)₄] (or 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O)

### Chlorides of Period 3
| Element | Chloride | Bonding | Reaction with water |
|---------|----------|---------|---------------------|
| Na | NaCl | Ionic | Dissolves, pH 7 |
| Mg | MgCl₂ | Ionic with covalent character | Dissolves, weakly acidic (pH ~6) |
| Al | AlCl₃ | Covalent (dimer Al₂Cl₆) | Hydrolyses, acidic (pH ~2–3), white fumes of HCl |
| Si | SiCl₄ | Covalent | Hydrolyses vigorously, white fumes of HCl, SiO₂ precipitate |
| P | PCl₅ / PCl₃ | Covalent | Hydrolyses, HCl fumes, H₃PO₄ / H₃PO₃ |
| S | S₂Cl₂ | Covalent | Hydrolyses |

> **Trend:** Ionic → covalent across period; hydrolysis becomes more vigorous

## 16. CHEMICAL EQUILIBRIUM (ACID-BASE)

### Brønsted-Lowry Theory
- **Acid:** Proton (H⁺) donor
- **Base:** Proton (H⁺) acceptor
- **Conjugate acid-base pair:** Differ by one H⁺

```
HA + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + A⁻
Acid    Base    Conjugate acid  Conjugate base
```

### Strong vs Weak Acids/Bases
| Type | Dissociation | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Strong acid** | Fully dissociates | HCl, H₂SO₄, HNO₃ |
| **Weak acid** | Partially dissociates | CH₃COOH, H₂CO₃ |
| **Strong base** | Fully dissociates | NaOH, KOH, Ba(OH)₂ |
| **Weak base** | Partially dissociates | NH₃, amines |

### pH Calculations
```
pH = –log[H⁺]
[H⁺] = 10^(–pH)
pOH = –log[OH⁻]
pH + pOH = 14   (at 25°C)
```

**Strong acid:** [H⁺] = acid concentration × number of H⁺ ions
**Strong base:** [OH⁻] = base concentration × number of OH⁻ ions; then use Kw

**Ionic product of water:**
```
Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 25°C
```

**Weak acid:**
```
Ka = [H⁺][A⁻] / [HA]
pKa = –log Ka
```
For weak acids where [HA]initial >> [H⁺]: [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka × [HA])

**Weak base:**
```
Kb = [BH⁺][OH⁻] / [B]
```

### Buffers
> Solutions that resist changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added

**Acidic buffer:** Weak acid + its conjugate base salt (e.g., CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa)
**Basic buffer:** Weak base + its conjugate acid salt (e.g., NH₃ + NH₄Cl)

**Henderson-Hasselbalch equation:**
```
pH = pKa + log ([A⁻] / [HA])
```

**How buffers work:**
- Added acid → reacts with A⁻ (conjugate base): A⁻ + H⁺ → HA
- Added base → reacts with HA (weak acid): HA + OH⁻ → A⁻ + H₂O

### Indicators
- Weak acids/bases where acid and conjugate base have **different colours**
- **pH range** = pKa ± 1 (colour change occurs over this range)

| Indicator | pH range | Acid colour | Alkali colour | Use |
|-----------|----------|-------------|---------------|-----|
| **Methyl orange** | 3.1–4.4 | Red | Yellow | Strong acid + strong/weak base |
| **Bromothymol blue** | 6.0–7.6 | Yellow | Blue | — |
| **Phenolphthalein** | 8.3–10.0 | Colourless | Pink | Weak acid + strong base |

### Titration Curves

| Titration type | pH at equivalence | Suitable indicator |
|----------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Strong acid + Strong base | 7 | Any |
| Weak acid + Strong base | >7 (basic salt) | Phenolphthalein |
| Strong acid + Weak base | <7 (acidic salt) | Methyl orange |
| Weak acid + Weak base | ~7 (steep portion less pronounced) | None suitable |

**Features of titration curve:**
- Initial pH depends on acid/base strength
- **Equivalence point:** Moles acid = moles base
- **Vertical region:** Steepest part of curve — indicator must change here
- **Half-equivalence point:** pH = pKa (for weak acid-strong base titration)

### Solubility Product (Ksp)
For: AₓBᵧ(s) ⇌ xAʸ⁺(aq) + yBˣ⁻(aq)
```
Ksp = [Aʸ⁺]ˣ [Bˣ⁻]ʸ
```
- Only applies to **saturated solutions**
- Units depend on stoichiometry
- **Common ion effect:** Adding a common ion decreases solubility (shifts equilibrium left)

**Predicting precipitation:**
- Calculate ionic product (Q) = [A]ˣ[B]ʸ
- Q > Ksp → precipitate forms
- Q = Ksp → saturated, equilibrium
- Q < Ksp → no precipitate, unsaturated

## EXAM TIPS

### General Advice
- **Always show working** in calculation questions — marks for method even if final answer is wrong
- **Check units** carefully: kJ vs J, dm³ vs cm³, g vs mg, kPa vs Pa
- **Use significant figures** appropriate to the data given (usually 3 s.f.)
- **Write balanced equations** with state symbols where asked
- **Read the question twice** — note whether it asks for atoms, molecules, moles, or mass

### Calculation Checklist
1. Convert volumes: cm³ → dm³ (÷1000)
2. Convert temperatures: °C → K (+273)
3. Moles = mass / Mr
4. Concentration = moles / volume (in dm³)
5. For gases at RTP: moles = volume (dm³) / 24
6. For ideal gas equation: ensure P in Pa, V in m³, T in K

### Organic Chemistry Tips
- **Name compounds carefully:** locate longest chain, number correctly, list substituents alphabetically
- **Draw displayed formulae** showing every atom and bond when asked
- **Mechanism arrows:** start from bond or lone pair, point to atom receiving electrons
- **Learn reagents and conditions** — these are easy marks
- **Distinguishing tests:** Know Tollens', Fehling's, bromine water, acidified KMnO₄, Na₂CO₃, etc.

### Inorganic Chemistry Tips
- **Learn colours of complex ions** and transition metal compounds
- **Know flame test colours:** Li (crimson), Na (yellow), K (lilac), Ca (brick red), Ba (apple green), Cu (blue-green)
- **Qualitative analysis:** Systematic approach — test for cations then anions
- **Trend explanations:** Always mention nuclear charge, atomic radius, shielding, and attraction for electrons

### Physical Chemistry Tips
- **Equilibrium:** Remember only temperature changes K; concentration/pressure changes shift position but not K
- **Electrochemistry:** E°cell > 0 = spontaneous; more positive = stronger oxidising agent
- **Kinetics:** Plot ln k vs 1/T for Arrhenius; gradient = –Ea/R
- **Enthalpy cycles:** Draw them clearly; label all arrows with substances and enthalpy changes

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Confusing **atomisation** with **ionisation**
- Forgetting to convert cm³ to dm³ in titration calculations
- Using molecular mass instead of molar mass in ideal gas equation
- Assuming all acids with H are strong (CH₃COOH is weak!)
- Drawing curly arrows incorrectly in mechanisms
- Forgetting that 3d fills after 4s but empties before 4s when forming ions
- Mixing up Kc and Kp (concentrations vs partial pressures)
- Confusing **electrophile** with **nucleophile**
- Using K₂Cr₂O₇ for oxidation without acid
- Forgetting that weak acids/bases do not fully dissociate

### Data Booklet Essentials
Know where to find:
- Standard electrode potentials
- Ionisation energies
- Bond enthalpies
- Characteristic IR absorptions
- ¹H NMR chemical shifts
- Acid dissociation constants (Ka)

### Time Management
- Start with questions you are most confident about
- Leave difficult calculations until the end if stuck
- Check all parts of multi-part questions are answered
- For extended response questions: plan your answer, use technical vocabulary, include equations/diagrams
