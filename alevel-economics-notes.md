# A-Level Economics Notes

## 1. BASIC ECONOMIC IDEAS

### The Economic Problem
> **Scarcity:** Unlimited wants + Limited resources = Need to make choices

### Factors of Production

| Factor | Description | Reward |
|--------|-------------|--------|
| **Land** | Natural resources | Rent |
| **Labour** | Human effort (mental + physical) | Wages |
| **Capital** | Man-made tools, machinery, buildings | Interest |
| **Enterprise** | Risk-taking, organising other factors | Profit |

### Opportunity Cost
> The **next best alternative foregone** when a choice is made.

### Production Possibility Curve (PPC)
- Shows maximum output combinations of two goods with given resources
- **Points on curve:** Efficient, full employment
- **Points inside curve:** Inefficient, unemployment
- **Points outside curve:** Unattainable with current resources
- **Outward shift:** Economic growth (more resources/technology)

## 2. ECONOMIC SYSTEMS

| System | What is produced? | How? | For whom? | Examples |
|--------|------------------|------|-----------|----------|
| **Free Market** | Consumer sovereignty | Private enterprise, profit motive | Those who can pay | Price mechanism |
| **Planned/Command** | Government decides | State-owned enterprises | Government priority | Cuba, North Korea |
| **Mixed** | Both market + government | Private + public sector | Combination | UK, USA, most countries |

### The Price Mechanism
```
High demand → Prices rise → Profits increase → Firms enter → Supply increases → Prices fall
Low demand → Prices fall → Profits decrease → Firms exit → Supply decreases → Prices rise
```

## 3. DEMAND & SUPPLY

### Demand
> Quantity of a good consumers are willing and able to buy at various prices.

**Law of Demand:** Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓ (inverse relationship)

**Reasons for downward-sloping demand curve:**
- Income effect (lower price = more purchasing power)
- Substitution effect (cheaper relative to alternatives)
- Diminishing marginal utility

**Determinants of Demand (shifts the curve):**
1. Income (normal goods ↑, inferior goods ↓)
2. Prices of substitutes (↑ price of substitute → ↑ demand)
3. Prices of complements (↑ price of complement → ↓ demand)
4. Tastes and preferences
5. Population/demographics
6. Expectations of future prices

### Supply
> Quantity of a good producers are willing and able to sell at various prices.

**Law of Supply:** Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑ (direct relationship)

**Determinants of Supply (shifts the curve):**
1. Costs of production (input prices, wages)
2. Technology
3. Number of sellers
4. Taxes/subsidies (taxes ↓ supply, subsidies ↑ supply)
5. Expectations of future prices
6. Weather/natural conditions (for agricultural products)

### Equilibrium
> Where demand = supply — market clears with no surplus or shortage.

**Surplus (Excess Supply):** Price above equilibrium → unsold stock → price falls
**Shortage (Excess Demand):** Price below equilibrium → queues/black markets → price rises

## 4. ELASTICITY

### Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

```
PED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Price)
```

| PED Value | Elasticity | Effect on Total Revenue |
|-----------|-----------|------------------------|
| PED > 1 | Elastic | Price ↑ → Revenue ↓ |
| PED = 1 | Unit elastic | Price ↑ → Revenue unchanged |
| PED < 1 | Inelastic | Price ↑ → Revenue ↑ |
| PED = 0 | Perfectly inelastic | Quantity fixed |
| PED = ∞ | Perfectly elastic | Any price change = zero demand |

**Determinants of PED:**
- Availability of substitutes
- Necessity vs luxury
- Proportion of income spent
- Time period (more elastic in long run)
- Habit-forming products (inelastic)

### Income Elasticity of Demand (YED)

```
YED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Income)
```

| YED Value | Type of Good |
|-----------|-------------|
| YED > 0 | Normal good |
| YED > 1 | Luxury good |
| 0 < YED < 1 | Necessity |
| YED < 0 | Inferior good |

### Cross Elasticity of Demand (XED)

```
XED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded of Good A) / (% Change in Price of Good B)
```

| XED Value | Relationship |
|-----------|-------------|
| XED > 0 | Substitutes |
| XED < 0 | Complements |
| XED = 0 | Unrelated |

### Price Elasticity of Supply (PES)

```
PES = (% Change in Quantity Supplied) / (% Change in Price)
```

| PES Value | Elasticity |
|-----------|-----------|
| PES > 1 | Elastic |
| PES < 1 | Inelastic |
| PES = 0 | Perfectly inelastic |
| PES = ∞ | Perfectly elastic |

**Determinants of PES:**
- Spare capacity
- Ease of factor substitution
- Time period (more elastic in long run)
- Availability of raw materials
- Mobility of factors

## 5. GOVERNMENT INTERVENTION

### Indirect Taxes

**Specific Tax:** Fixed amount per unit (shifts supply curve up parallel)
**Ad Valorem Tax:** Percentage of price (rotates supply curve)

**Incidence of Tax:**
- More burden on side that is more inelastic
- PED < PES → Consumers bear more tax
- PED > PES → Producers bear more tax

**Effects:**
- Higher price for consumers
- Lower price received by producers
- Reduced quantity traded
- Tax revenue for government
- Deadweight loss (welfare loss)

### Subsidies
- Government payment to producers/consumers
- Shifts supply curve down
- Increases quantity, lowers consumer price
- Cost to government

### Maximum Price (Price Ceiling)
- Set below equilibrium
- Causes shortage
- Leads to queues, black markets, rationing
- Example: Rent controls

### Minimum Price (Price Floor)
- Set above equilibrium
- Causes surplus
- Government may need to buy excess supply
- Example: Minimum wage, agricultural price supports

### Buffer Stock Schemes
- Buy when price falls below floor
- Sell when price rises above ceiling
- Stabilize prices for producers
- Requires storage, funding, accurate forecasts

## 6. MARKET FAILURE

### Types of Market Failure

#### 1. Externalities
> Costs or benefits affecting third parties not involved in the transaction.

| Type | Effect | Example |
|------|--------|---------|
| **Negative Production Externality** | MSC > MPC | Pollution from factory |
| **Negative Consumption Externality** | MSB < MPB | Smoking, drink driving |
| **Positive Production Externality** | MSC < MPC | Beekeeping (pollination) |
| **Positive Consumption Externality** | MSB > MPB | Vaccinations, education |

**Social Optimum:** Where MSC = MSB
**Private Market:** Where MPC = MPB

**Government Solutions for Externalities:**
- Taxes (Pigouvian tax = external cost)
- Subsidies (for positive externalities)
- Regulation/standards
- Tradable permits
- Property rights (Coase theorem)

#### 2. Public Goods
- **Non-excludable:** Cannot prevent non-payers from consuming
- **Non-rivalrous:** One person's consumption doesn't reduce availability
- **Free-rider problem:** Private sector won't provide
- Examples: National defense, street lighting, lighthouses
- Government must provide through taxation

#### 3. Merit & Demerit Goods

| | Merit Goods | Demerit Goods |
|---|-------------|---------------|
| **Definition** | Under-consumed socially beneficial goods | Over-consumed socially harmful goods |
| **Problem** | Information failure, positive externalities | Information failure, negative externalities |
| **Examples** | Education, healthcare, vaccinations | Cigarettes, alcohol, drugs |
| **Government Action** | Subsidies, free provision, information campaigns | Taxes, bans, age limits, warnings |

#### 4. Information Failure
- Asymmetric information (one party knows more)
- Adverse selection (bad products drive out good)
- Moral hazard (change behaviour after transaction)

#### 5. Market Power
- Monopoly/oligopoly restrict output, raise prices
- Allocative inefficiency (P > MC)
- Need competition policy/regulation

## 7. THEORY OF THE FIRM

### Costs of Production

| Cost | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Fixed Costs (TFC)** | Don't vary with output (rent, salaries) |
| **Variable Costs (TVC)** | Vary with output (raw materials, labour) |
| **Total Cost (TC)** | TFC + TVC |
| **Average Fixed Cost (AFC)** | TFC / Q |
| **Average Variable Cost (AVC)** | TVC / Q |
| **Average Total Cost (ATC)** | TC / Q = AFC + AVC |
| **Marginal Cost (MC)** | Change in TC from producing one more unit |

**MC intersects AVC and ATC at their minimum points.**

### Revenue

| Revenue | Definition |
|---------|-----------|
| **Total Revenue (TR)** | Price × Quantity |
| **Average Revenue (AR)** | TR / Q = Price |
| **Marginal Revenue (MR)** | Change in TR from selling one more unit |

### Profit Maximization
> Firms maximize profit where **MC = MR**

**Types of Profit:**
- **Supernormal/Abnormal Profit:** AR > ATC
- **Normal Profit:** AR = ATC (minimum to stay in industry)
- **Subnormal/Loss:** AR < ATC

### Shut-Down Point
- Short run: Shut down if AR < AVC
- Long run: Exit if AR < ATC

## 8. MARKET STRUCTURES

### Perfect Competition

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Number of firms | Many |
| Product | Homogeneous/identical |
| Barriers to entry | None |
| Information | Perfect |
| Price control | None (price taker) |

**Equilibrium:**
- Short run: Can make supernormal profit or loss
- Long run: Only normal profit (firms enter/exit until AR = ATC)
- Productive efficiency: Min ATC
- Allocative efficiency: P = MC

### Monopoly

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Number of firms | One |
| Barriers to entry | High (legal, natural, strategic) |
| Price control | Price maker |

**Characteristics:**
- Higher price, lower output than perfect competition
- Supernormal profits in long run
- No productive or allocative efficiency (P > MC, not at min ATC)
- May have economies of scale
- May benefit from innovation (supernormal profits fund R&D)

**Natural Monopoly:** Single firm can supply entire market at lower cost than multiple firms (high fixed costs)

### Monopolistic Competition

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Number of firms | Many |
| Product | Differentiated |
| Barriers to entry | Low |

**Characteristics:**
- Downward-sloping demand curve (some price control)
- Non-price competition (branding, quality)
- Short run: Can make supernormal profit
- Long run: Only normal profit (AR tangent to ATC)
- Excess capacity (not at minimum ATC)

### Oligopoly

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| Number of firms | Few dominant firms |
| Barriers to entry | High |
| Interdependence | Firms react to rivals' actions |

**Models:**
- **Kinked Demand Curve:** Rivals match price cuts but not price rises → price rigidity
- **Game Theory (Prisoner's Dilemma):** Collusion vs competition
- **Cartels:** Firms act as monopoly (illegal in most countries)

**Non-Price Competition:** Advertising, branding, loyalty schemes, after-sales service

## 9. LABOUR MARKET

### Demand for Labour
> Derived demand — demanded for what they produce.

**Determinants:**
- Demand for final product
- Productivity of labour
- Wage rate
- Price of capital (substitution/complementary effects)

### Supply of Labour

**Determinants:**
- Wage rate (backward-bending supply curve possible)
- Population/demographics
- Migration
- Preferences for leisure vs work
- Non-wage factors (job satisfaction, flexibility)

### Wage Determination

**Perfectly Competitive Labour Market:**
- Firms are wage takers
- Wage = MRP (Marginal Revenue Product)
- MRP = MPP × MR

**Monopsony (Single Buyer):**
- Firm has buying power
- Employs where MRP = MLC (Marginal Labour Cost)
- Wage < MRP → Exploitation
- Minimum wage can increase employment and wages

**Trade Unions:**
- Bargain for higher wages, better conditions
- Methods: Collective bargaining, industrial action, closed shops
- Effect depends on elasticity of labour demand

### Transfer Earnings vs Economic Rent
- **Transfer Earnings:** Minimum payment needed to keep factor in current use
- **Economic Rent:** Payment above transfer earnings
- Inelastic supply → more economic rent

## 10. MACROECONOMIC OBJECTIVES

### The Five Main Objectives

| Objective | Target | Measured By |
|-----------|--------|-------------|
| **Economic Growth** | Sustainable increase in output | Real GDP, GDP per capita |
| **Low Unemployment** | Full employment | Unemployment rate |
| **Low Inflation** | Stable prices (≈2% target) | CPI, RPI |
| **Balance of Payments** | Sustainable current account | Current account balance |
| **Redistribution of Income** | Reduced inequality | Gini coefficient, Lorenz curve |

**Trade-offs:**
- Phillips Curve: Short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment
- Growth vs environment
- Growth vs income inequality
- Current account vs domestic demand

## 11. MEASURING ECONOMIC PERFORMANCE

### GDP (Gross Domestic Product)
> Total value of goods and services produced within a country's borders in a time period.

**Methods of Calculation:**
1. **Expenditure Approach:** GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)
   - C = Consumer spending
   - I = Investment
   - G = Government spending
   - X = Exports
   - M = Imports

2. **Income Approach:** Sum of all incomes (wages, profits, rent, interest)

3. **Output Approach:** Sum of value added at each stage

**Real vs Nominal GDP:**
- Nominal: Current prices
- Real: Constant prices (adjusted for inflation)
- Real GDP growth = Economic growth

**GDP per Capita:** GDP / Population (standard of living indicator)

### Limitations of GDP
- Ignores income distribution
- Ignores environmental costs
- Ignores informal/black economy
- Doesn't measure happiness/well-being
- Doesn't account for leisure time
- Ignores depreciation of capital

### Alternative Measures
- **HDI (Human Development Index):** GDP per capita, life expectancy, education
- **Gini Coefficient:** Income inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality)
- **Lorenz Curve:** Graphical representation of income distribution
- **MPI (Multidimensional Poverty Index):** Health, education, living standards

## 12. INFLATION

### Definitions
> **Inflation:** Sustained increase in general price level.
> **Deflation:** Sustained decrease in general price level.
> **Disinflation:** Inflation rate decreasing but still positive.

### Measuring Inflation

**CPI (Consumer Price Index):**
- Basket of goods weighted by household spending patterns
- Base year = 100
- Compare cost of basket over time

```
Inflation Rate = ((CPI Current - CPI Previous) / CPI Previous) × 100
```

**RPI (Retail Price Index):** Includes housing costs (mortgage interest)

### Types of Inflation

| Type | Cause |
|------|-------|
| **Demand-Pull** | AD > AS → "too much money chasing too few goods" |
| **Cost-Push** | Rising costs (wages, raw materials) shift SRAS left |
| **Built-in/Wage-Price Spiral** | Expectations of inflation → wage demands → price rises |
| **Imported** | Rising prices of imports (currency depreciation) |
| **Monetary** | Excessive money supply growth |

### Costs of Inflation
- Menu costs (changing prices)
- Shoe leather costs (searching for best prices)
- Redistribution (fixed income losers, debtors gain)
- Uncertainty reduces investment
- Menu costs for international competitiveness
- Breakdown of money as store of value

### Benefits of Mild Inflation
- Encourages spending (rather than hoarding)
- Reduces real burden of debt
- Allows relative price adjustments without nominal cuts
- Avoids deflation spiral

## 13. UNEMPLOYMENT

### Types of Unemployment

| Type | Cause | Solution |
|------|-------|----------|
| **Frictional** | Time between jobs | Better job information |
| **Structural** | Skills/location mismatch | Retraining, relocation |
| **Cyclical (Demand-Deficient)** | Recession, low AD | Fiscal/monetary stimulus |
| **Seasonal** | Seasonal industries | Diversification |
| **Technological** | Automation replaces labour | Retraining, education |
| **Voluntary** | Choose not to work at current wage | Incentives to work |

### Natural Rate of Unemployment (NRU)
> Unemployment when economy is at full employment — only frictional + structural.

### Phillips Curve
- **Short-run:** Inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment
- **Long-run:** Vertical at NRU (no trade-off — expectations adjust)

### Costs of Unemployment
- Lost output (GDP below potential)
- Fiscal costs (benefits, lost tax revenue)
- Social costs (poverty, crime, mental health)
- Human capital depreciation
- Hysteresis (long-term unemployed lose skills)

## 14. AGGREGATE DEMAND (AD)

```
AD = C + I + G + (X - M)
```

### Components

| Component | Determinants |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Consumption (C)** | Income, wealth, interest rates, consumer confidence, expectations |
| **Investment (I)** | Interest rates, business confidence, technology, expectations, profits |
| **Government Spending (G)** | Political priorities, automatic stabilizers |
| **Net Exports (X-M)** | Exchange rates, foreign income, trade barriers, competitiveness |

### AD Curve
- Downward sloping (higher price level → lower real spending)
- Shifts right: Expansionary fiscal/monetary policy, increased confidence
- Shifts left: Contractionary policy, decreased confidence, recession abroad

---

## 15. AGGREGATE SUPPLY (AS)

### Short-Run Aggregate Supply (SRAS)
- Upward sloping (higher prices → firms supply more)
- Shifts right: Lower input costs, improved productivity, subsidies
- Shifts left: Higher costs, taxes, supply shocks

### Long-Run Aggregate Supply (LRAS)
- Vertical at full employment output
- Determined by: Quantity/quality of factors of production, technology
- Shifts right: Economic growth (more/better resources)

### Keynesian vs Classical AS
- **Classical:** LRAS vertical even in short run (markets clear quickly)
- **Keynesian:** SRAS may be horizontal at low output (sticky wages/prices)

## 16. ECONOMIC GROWTH

### Types
- **Actual Growth:** Increase in real GDP (movement along PPC)
- **Potential Growth:** Increase in productive capacity (shift of PPC/LRAS)

### Causes of Growth
- Increase in quantity of factors (labour, capital, land)
- Increase in quality of factors (education, technology)
- Improved efficiency (better resource allocation)

### Benefits
- Higher living standards
- More tax revenue for government
- Reduced unemployment
- Investment in infrastructure/technology

### Costs
- Environmental degradation
- Resource depletion
- Income inequality
- Loss of leisure time
- Inflationary pressure
- Current account deficits (if consumption of imports rises)

### Sustainable Growth
- Meets needs of present without compromising future
- Considers environmental and social factors
- Green growth: Decoupling growth from environmental damage

## 17. FISCAL POLICY

### Definition
> Government use of **spending** and **taxation** to influence the economy.

### Expansionary Fiscal Policy (Boost AD)
- Increase government spending (G ↑)
- Decrease taxes (C and I ↑)
- Budget deficit or reduced surplus

### Contractionary Fiscal Policy (Reduce AD)
- Decrease government spending (G ↓)
- Increase taxes (C and I ↓)
- Budget surplus or reduced deficit

### Budget Outcomes
- **Balanced Budget:** G = T
- **Budget Deficit:** G > T (government borrows)
- **Budget Surplus:** G < T (government repays debt)
- **Primary Deficit:** Excluding interest payments on debt

### Automatic Stabilizers
> Built-in features that stabilize economy without policy changes.
- **Progressive taxes:** Tax revenue falls in recession (supports income)
- **Unemployment benefits:** Spending rises in recession (supports demand)

### Discretionary Fiscal Policy
> Deliberate changes in G or T by government decision.

### Multiplier Effect
```
Multiplier (k) = 1 / (1 - MPC) = 1 / MPS
```
- Initial injection of spending leads to larger total increase in AD
- Size depends on MPC (Marginal Propensity to Consume)
- Leakages: Savings, taxes, imports reduce multiplier

### Crowding Out
- Government borrowing raises interest rates
- Reduces private sector investment
- May offset expansionary effect

## 18. MONETARY POLICY

### Definition
> Central bank control of **money supply** and **interest rates** to influence the economy.

### Tools

| Tool | Expansionary | Contractionary |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| **Interest Rates** | Lower rates | Raise rates |
| **Quantitative Easing (QE)** | Buy government bonds | Sell government bonds |
| **Reserve Requirements** | Lower reserves | Raise reserves |

### Transmission Mechanism
```
Interest rate change → Cost of borrowing → Consumption/Investment → AD → Output/Employment/Inflation
```

### Interest Rate Effects
| | Lower Rates | Higher Rates |
|---|-------------|--------------|
| Borrowing | Cheaper | More expensive |
| Saving | Less attractive | More attractive |
| Exchange Rate | Depreciates | Appreciates |
| Asset Prices | Rise | Fall |
| AD | Increases | Decreases |

### Limitations
- Liquidity trap (rates near zero, saving still high)
- Time lags
- Uncertainty about magnitude
- Conflicts with other objectives
- Effectiveness depends on confidence

### Quantitative Easing (QE)
- Central bank buys financial assets (government bonds)
- Increases money supply
- Lowers long-term interest rates
- Used when conventional policy exhausted (zero lower bound)

## 19. SUPPLY-SIDE POLICIES

### Definition
> Policies to increase productive capacity / shift LRAS right.

### Market-Based Policies

| Policy | How it Works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Privatization** | Sell state-owned enterprises → efficiency, competition |
| **Deregulation** | Remove barriers to entry/competition |
| **Labour Market Reform** | Reduce union power, minimum wages, welfare reform |
| **Tax Cuts** | Incentivize work, investment, entrepreneurship |
| **Free Trade** | Remove tariffs/quotas → specialisation, competition |

### Interventionist Policies

| Policy | How it Works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Education/Training** | Improve human capital |
| **Infrastructure Investment** | Transport, communications, energy |
| **R&D Support** | Grants, tax incentives for innovation |
| **Industrial Policy** | Support key/strategic industries |

### Evaluation
- Long time lags
- Political constraints
- May increase inequality
- Market-based: May fail if markets not competitive
- Interventionist: Government failure, opportunity cost

## 20. INTERNATIONAL TRADE

### Reasons for Trade
- **Absolute Advantage:** Can produce more with same resources
- **Comparative Advantage:** Lower opportunity cost of production

**Comparative Advantage Example:**
```
Country A: 1 cloth = 0.5 wine
Country B: 1 cloth = 2 wine
→ Country A has comparative advantage in cloth
→ Country B has comparative advantage in wine
→ Both gain from specialisation and trade
```

### Free Trade vs Protectionism

**Arguments for Free Trade:**
- Lower prices for consumers
- Greater choice
- Economies of scale
- Specialisation by comparative advantage
- Competition drives efficiency
- Growth and development

**Arguments for Protectionism:**
- Protect infant industries
- Protect domestic employment
- Prevent dumping
- National security
- Diversification
- Retaliation

### Protectionist Methods

| Method | How it Works |
|--------|-------------|
| **Tariffs** | Tax on imports → higher domestic price |
| **Quotas** | Limit on quantity of imports |
| **Subsidies** | Domestic producers get government support |
| **Embargoes** | Complete ban on trade |
| **Administrative Barriers** | Regulations, standards, paperwork |
| **Exchange Controls** | Limit foreign currency for imports |

### Trade Organisations
- **WTO:** Promotes free trade, settles disputes
- **Trading Blocs:** Free trade areas, customs unions, common markets
- **EU, USMCA, ASEAN, etc.**

## 21. BALANCE OF PAYMENTS

### Current Account

| Component | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Trade in Goods** | Visible balance (exports - imports) |
| **Trade in Services** | Invisible balance (exports - imports) |
| **Primary Income** | Wages, profits, interest, dividends |
| **Secondary Income** | Transfers (aid, remittances, grants) |

```
Current Account = (X - M) + Net Income + Net Transfers
```

### Capital Account
- Capital transfers
- Acquisition/disposal of non-produced assets

### Financial Account
- Direct investment
- Portfolio investment
- Reserve assets
- Other investment

> **Current Account + Capital Account + Financial Account = 0** (with errors/omissions)

### Causes of Current Account Deficit
- Overvalued exchange rate
- Low competitiveness
- High inflation
- Strong domestic demand (imports)
- Structural decline in manufacturing
- Dependence on imports (energy, food)

### Consequences of Deficit
- Foreign debt accumulation
- Currency depreciation pressure
- Reduced foreign reserves
- May require austerity

### Policies to Reduce Deficit
- Expenditure-switching (devalue currency, tariffs)
- Expenditure-reducing (contractionary fiscal/monetary)
- Supply-side (improve competitiveness)
- Protectionism

## 22. EXCHANGE RATES

### Exchange Rate Systems

| System | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Fixed** | Pegged to another currency/gold/basket |
| **Floating** | Determined by market forces (D&S for currency) |
| **Managed/Dirty Float** | Mostly market-determined with occasional intervention |

### Factors Affecting Exchange Rates
- Interest rate differentials
- Inflation differentials
- Current account position
- Speculation
- Economic growth
- Political stability
- Central bank intervention

### Effects of Exchange Rate Changes

**Currency Appreciation:**
- Cheaper imports
- More expensive exports
- Lower inflation (import prices fall)
- Worsens current account (if Marshall-Lerner condition not met)
- Reduces competitiveness

**Currency Depreciation:**
- More expensive imports
- Cheaper exports
- Higher inflation
- Improves current account (if Marshall-Lerner condition met)
- Increases competitiveness

### Marshall-Lerner Condition
> Depreciation improves current account if **PED of exports + PED of imports > 1**

**J-Curve Effect:**
- Short run: Current account worsens (inelastic, contracts fixed)
- Long run: Current account improves (elasticities increase)

## 23. DEVELOPMENT ECONOMICS

### Development Indicators
- GDP per capita (PPP)
- HDI
- Life expectancy
- Literacy rates
- Access to healthcare/education

- Poor infrastructure
- Lack of human capital
- Debt burden
- Primary product dependency
- Trade barriers from developed countries
- Climate change/environmental degradation

### Development Strategies

| Strategy | Approach |
|----------|----------|
| **Import Substitution** | Replace imports with domestic production |
| **Export Orientation** | Focus on manufactured exports |
| **Microfinance** | Small loans to entrepreneurs |
| **Fair Trade** | Better prices for producers in developing countries |
| **Foreign Aid** | Grants/loans from developed countries |
| **FDI** | Foreign direct investment |

### Role of International Organisations
- **IMF:** Balance of payments support, macroeconomic stability
- **World Bank:** Long-term development loans/projects
- **WTO:** Trade rules and liberalisation

## EXAM TIPS

1. **Use diagrams** — Always draw AD/AS, demand/supply, PPC diagrams where relevant
2. **Define key terms** — Start with clear definitions
3. **Apply to context** — Use the data/extract provided in the question
4. **Evaluate** — Consider both sides, use "however," "on the other hand"
5. **Chain of reasoning** — Explain step-by-step (e.g., interest rate → borrowing → investment → AD → GDP)
6. **Real-world examples** — Support with current or historical examples
7. **Policy evaluation** — Consider time lags, conflicts, effectiveness
8. **Don't just list** — Explain *why* and *how*
9. **Watch the command words** — Explain vs evaluate vs discuss vs analyse
10. **Time management** — Don't run out of time for the final question
