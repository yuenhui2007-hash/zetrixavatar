## 1. Cell Structure

### 1.1 Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| **Nucleus** | No true nucleus; naked DNA in nucleoid | True nucleus with nuclear envelope |
| **DNA** | Circular, naked | Linear, associated with histones |
| **Ribosomes** | 70S (smaller) | 80S (larger) |
| **Membrane-bound organelles** | Absent | Present |
| **Cell wall** | Peptidoglycan (murein) | Cellulose (plants), chitin (fungi), absent (animals) |
| **Size** | 0.1-5.0 μm | 10-100 μm |
| **Examples** | Bacteria, Archaea | Plants, animals, fungi, protists |

**Key distinction:** Prokaryotes have **no membrane-bound organelles** and **no true nucleus**.

### 1.2 Organelles

| Organelle | Structure | Function |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| **Nucleus** | Double membrane (nuclear envelope) with pores; contains chromatin and nucleolus | Contains genetic material; controls cell activities; site of ribosome synthesis |
| **Nucleolus** | Dense region inside nucleus | Produces rRNA; assembles ribosomes |
| **Mitochondria** | Double membrane; inner membrane folded into cristae; matrix contains DNA and ribosomes | **Aerobic respiration**; produces ATP (site of Krebs cycle and ETC) |
| **Ribosomes** | Two subunits (large and small); made of rRNA and protein | **Protein synthesis** (translation) |
| **Rough ER** | Membrane-bound flattened sacs with ribosomes attached | Synthesises and transports proteins; modifies proteins |
| **Smooth ER** | Membrane-bound tubular network without ribosomes | Synthesises lipids and steroids; detoxification; calcium ion storage |
| **Golgi apparatus** | Stack of flattened membrane-bound sacs (cisternae) | Modifies, packages, and sorts proteins and lipids into vesicles |
| **Lysosomes** | Spherical sacs containing hydrolytic enzymes | Digests worn-out organelles, pathogens, and macromolecules |
| **Chloroplasts** | Double membrane; thylakoids stacked into grana; stroma contains DNA and ribosomes | **Photosynthesis** |
| **Vacuole** | Membrane-bound sac (tonoplast) | Storage (water, ions, pigments); maintains turgor pressure |
| **Cell wall** | Rigid outer layer | Structural support and protection |
| **Plasma membrane** | Phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins | Controls entry and exit of substances |
| **Centrioles** | Pair of cylindrical structures made of microtubules | Form spindle fibres during cell division |
| **Cytoskeleton** | Network of microtubules, microfilaments, intermediate filaments | Structural support; cell movement; intracellular transport |

### 1.3 Microscopy

**Light Microscope**
- Maximum resolution: **~200 nm**
- Maximum magnification: **×1500**
- Uses visible light and glass lenses
- Specimen may be stained (e.g., iodine, methylene blue)
- Living specimens can be viewed

**Electron Microscope (TEM)**
- Maximum resolution: **~0.2 nm**
- Maximum magnification: **×500,000**
- Uses electron beam and electromagnetic lenses
- **Transmission EM (TEM):** 2D image, thin sections, high resolution
- **Scanning EM (SEM):** 3D surface image, lower resolution than TEM
- Specimen must be in a vacuum (cannot view living cells)
- Stained with heavy metals (electron-dense)

**Resolution vs Magnification**
- **Resolution:** Ability to distinguish two separate points as distinct. Limited by wavelength of radiation.
- **Magnification:** How much larger the image appears than the actual object.

$$\text{Magnification} = \frac{\text{Image size}}{\text{Actual size}}$$

**Standard form conversions:**
- 1 mm = 10⁻³ m
- 1 μm = 10⁻⁶ m
- 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m

## 2. Biological Molecules

### 2.1 Carbohydrates

**Monosaccharides** (simple sugars, formula C₆H₁₂O₆)
- **Glucose:** α-glucose and β-glucose (structural isomers)
- **Fructose:** sweetest natural sugar
- **Galactose:** component of lactose
- **Ribose:** C₅H₁₀O₅, found in RNA
- **Deoxyribose:** C₅H₁₀O₄, found in DNA

**Disaccharides** (condensation reaction, glycosidic bond)
| Disaccharide | Monomers | Glycosidic bond |
|--------------|----------|-----------------|
| **Maltose** | Glucose + Glucose | α-1,4 |
| **Sucrose** | Glucose + Fructose | α-1,β-2 |
| **Lactose** | Glucose + Galactose | β-1,4 |

**Polysaccharides**
| Polysaccharide | Monomer | Bond | Function | Features |
|----------------|---------|------|----------|----------|
| **Starch** | α-glucose | α-1,4 and α-1,6 | Energy storage in plants | Compact, insoluble, helical amylose, branched amylopectin |
| **Glycogen** | α-glucose | α-1,4 and α-1,6 | Energy storage in animals | Highly branched; rapid glucose release |
| **Cellulose** | β-glucose | β-1,4 | Structural support in plants | Straight chains; hydrogen bonds form microfibrils |
| **Chitin** | N-acetylglucosamine | β-1,4 | Structural support in fungi and arthropods | Contains nitrogen |

**Condensation vs Hydrolysis**
- **Condensation:** Forms glycosidic bond; releases H₂O
- **Hydrolysis:** Breaks glycosidic bond; requires H₂O

### 2.2 Lipids

**Triglycerides**
- One **glycerol** + three **fatty acids**
- Ester bond formed via condensation
- **Saturated:** No C=C bonds; solid at room temperature
- **Unsaturated:** One or more C=C bonds; liquid at room temperature
- Energy storage, insulation, protection

**Phospholipids**
- One glycerol + two fatty acids + one phosphate group
- **Amphipathic:** Hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tails
- Form bilayers in aqueous environments

**Cholesterol**
- Small, hydrophobic molecule
- Fits between phospholipids in membranes
- Decreases fluidity at high temperatures; prevents crystallisation at low temperatures
- Precursor for steroid hormones, vitamin D, bile salts

**Test for Lipids:** Emulsion test — ethanol + water → white emulsion

### 2.3 Proteins

**Amino acids** — basic monomers
- General formula: central carbon bonded to H, NH₂, COOH, and R group
- **20 different R groups** → 20 amino acids
- **Dipeptide:** Two amino acids joined by **peptide bond** (condensation)
- **Polypeptide:** Chain of many amino acids

**Protein Structure**
| Level | Description | Bonds |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| **Primary** | Sequence of amino acids | Peptide bonds |
| **Secondary** | α-helix or β-pleated sheet | Hydrogen bonds |
| **Tertiary** | 3D folding of polypeptide | Hydrogen, ionic, disulfide bridges, hydrophobic interactions |
| **Quaternary** | Multiple polypeptide chains | Same as tertiary |

**Globular proteins** (e.g., enzymes, antibodies, haemoglobin) — soluble, rounded
**Fibrous proteins** (e.g., collagen, keratin) — structural, insoluble

**Tests for Proteins:**
- **Biuret test:** NaOH + CuSO₄ → purple/violet = protein present

### 2.4 Nucleic Acids

**DNA Structure (Watson-Crick model)**
- Double helix of two **antiparallel** polynucleotide strands
- **Sugar-phosphate backbone** (deoxyribose + phosphate)
- **Bases:** Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Cytosine (C), Guanine (G)
- **Complementary base pairing:** A=T (2 hydrogen bonds), C≡G (3 hydrogen bonds)
- **Purines** (A, G) = double ring; **Pyrimidines** (C, T) = single ring

**RNA**
- Single-stranded
- Ribose sugar (not deoxyribose)
- Uracil (U) replaces Thymine
- Types: **mRNA, tRNA, rRNA**

### 2.5 Water

**Properties essential for life:**
- **Solvent:** Dissolves polar/ionic substances
- **High specific heat capacity:** Resists temperature changes
- **High latent heat of vaporisation:** Cooling via evaporation
- **Cohesion and adhesion:** Water transport in xylem
- **Density anomaly:** Ice floats (insulates water below)
- **Metabolic reactant:** Hydrolysis and photosynthesis

### 2.6 Enzyme Basics

- **Globular proteins** that act as **biological catalysts**
- Lower activation energy (Eₐ) without being used up
- **Active site:** Specific 3D region where substrate binds
- **Lock and key model** → refined to **induced fit model**

## 3. Enzymes

### 3.1 Mechanism of Action

**Induced Fit Model**
1. Active site is **complementary** but not perfectly rigid
2. Substrate binds → enzyme changes shape slightly
3. **Transition state** stabilised → bonds in substrate strain
4. Products formed → released; enzyme returns to original shape

**Lowering Activation Energy**
- Enzymes provide an **alternative reaction pathway**
- Hold substrates in correct orientation
- Form temporary bonds with substrate (ES complex)
- Stabilise transition state

### 3.2 Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

**Temperature**
- Rate increases with temperature (more kinetic energy, more collisions)
- **Optimum temperature** (~37°C for human enzymes)
- Above optimum: bonds break, enzyme **denatures**
- Denaturation = permanent loss of 3D structure and active site shape

**pH**
- Each enzyme has an **optimum pH**
- Extreme pH alters ionisation of R groups → changes active site shape
- Most enzymes: optimum pH ~7
- Pepsin: pH ~2 (stomach); Trypsin: pH ~8 (small intestine)

**Substrate Concentration**
- Rate increases with [S] up to a point
- **Vmax** = maximum rate when all active sites saturated
- At Vmax, rate limited by enzyme concentration

**Enzyme Concentration**
- Directly proportional to rate (assuming excess substrate)

### 3.3 Inhibition

**Competitive Inhibition**
- Inhibitor has similar shape to substrate
- Binds reversibly to **active site**
- Increases apparent Km; Vmax unchanged
- Can be overcome by increasing [S]
- Example: Malonate inhibits succinate dehydrogenase

**Non-competitive Inhibition**
- Inhibitor binds to **allosteric site** (not active site)
- Changes shape of active site
- Cannot be overcome by increasing [S]
- Decreases Vmax; Km unchanged
- Example: Cyanide inhibits cytochrome oxidase

**End-product Inhibition (Feedback Inhibition)**
- End product inhibits an earlier enzyme in the pathway
- Prevents overproduction; maintains homeostasis

### 3.4 Industrial Uses

| Application | Enzyme | Use |
|-------------|--------|-----|
| **Biological detergents** | Proteases, lipases, amylases | Break down stains |
| **Food industry** | Pectinase | Clarify fruit juice |
| | Protease (rennin) | Cheese making |
| | Amylase, glucose isomerase | High fructose corn syrup |
| **Medicine** | Streptokinase | Dissolve blood clots |
| **Diagnostics** | Glucose oxidase | Blood glucose test strips |

**Immobilised Enzymes**
- Enzymes trapped in alginate beads or attached to surfaces
- Advantages: reusable, more stable, product not contaminated, continuous processing possible

## 4. Cell Membranes

### 4.1 Fluid Mosaic Model

**Components:**
- **Phospholipid bilayer:** Hydrophobic interior, hydrophilic exterior
- **Integral proteins:** Span the membrane (channels, carriers, pumps)
- **Peripheral proteins:** Attached to surface
- **Cholesterol:** Fits between phospholipids; regulates fluidity
- **Glycoproteins:** Proteins with carbohydrate chains; cell recognition, antigens
- **Glycolipids:** Lipids with carbohydrate chains; cell recognition

**Fluidity factors:**
- Temperature ↑ → fluidity ↑
- Saturated fatty acids ↓ → fluidity ↓
- Unsaturated fatty acids ↑ → fluidity ↑
- Cholesterol ↓ → fluidity ↑ (at normal temperatures)

### 4.2 Transport Across Membranes

**Diffusion**
- Movement of molecules from high to low concentration
- Passive (no ATP required)
- **Simple diffusion:** Through phospholipid bilayer (small, non-polar)
- **Facilitated diffusion:** Through channel/carrier proteins (polar, charged, large)
- Rate depends on: concentration gradient, surface area, temperature, membrane thickness

**Osmosis**
- Diffusion of **water** across a partially permeable membrane
- From high water potential to low water potential
- **Water potential (Ψ):** Pressure + solute potential
- Pure water: Ψ = 0 kPa (highest)
- Adding solutes: lowers water potential (becomes negative)

**Active Transport**
- Movement against concentration gradient
- Requires **ATP** and **carrier proteins**
- Example: Na⁺/K⁺ pump, glucose absorption in ileum

**Endocytosis and Exocytosis**
- **Endocytosis:** Bulk uptake; membrane invaginates → vesicle forms
  - Phagocytosis (solids) → phagosome → lysosome fusion
  - Pinocytosis (liquids)
- **Exocytosis:** Bulk export; vesicle fuses with membrane → releases contents

## 5. Cell Division

### 5.1 The Cell Cycle

**Interphase** (longest phase)
- **G₁ phase:** Cell growth, protein synthesis, organelle replication
- **S phase:** DNA replication
- **G₂ phase:** Further growth, synthesis of proteins for division

**Mitosis** (M phase)

### 5.2 Mitosis

Produces **two genetically identical diploid daughter cells**.
Used for growth, repair, asexual reproduction.

| Stage | Key Events |
|-------|------------|
| **Prophase** | Chromosomes condense (become visible); nuclear envelope breaks down; centrioles move to poles; spindle forms |
| **Metaphase** | Chromosomes line up on equator; spindle fibres attach to centromeres |
| **Anaphase** | Centromeres divide; sister chromatids pulled to opposite poles |
| **Telophase** | Chromatids decondense; nuclear envelope reforms; spindle breaks down; cytokinesis begins |

**Cytokinesis:** Cytoplasm divides → two daughter cells

### 5.3 Meiosis

Produces **four genetically different haploid gametes**.
Used for sexual reproduction; introduces genetic variation.

**Meiosis I (Reduction division)**
- **Prophase I:** Homologous chromosomes pair up (**synapsis**) → **bivalent**; **crossing over** occurs (chiasmata form)
- **Metaphase I:** Bivalents line up on equator
- **Anaphase I:** Homologous chromosomes separate → poles
- **Telophase I:** Two haploid cells formed

**Meiosis II (Equational division)** — similar to mitosis
- Sister chromatids separate
- Four haploid cells produced

**Sources of Genetic Variation in Meiosis:**
1. **Crossing over** (prophase I) — exchange of genetic material
2. **Independent assortment** (metaphase I) — random orientation of bivalents
3. **Random fertilisation** — any gamete can fuse with any other

### 5.4 Cancer

- **Uncontrolled cell division** due to mutations in genes regulating the cell cycle
- **Oncogenes:** Mutated versions of proto-oncogenes; stimulate cell division
- **Tumour suppressor genes:** Normally inhibit cell division; when mutated, division unchecked
- **Benign tumour:** Encapsulated, does not invade surrounding tissue, not cancerous
- **Malignant tumour:** Invades surrounding tissue, can metastasise (spread via blood/lymph)
- **Carcinogens:** Agents that cause cancer (e.g., UV radiation, tobacco smoke, asbestos)

## 6. Genetic Information

### 6.1 DNA Structure and Replication

**DNA Replication (Semi-conservative)**
1. **Helicase** unwinds and unzips DNA (breaks hydrogen bonds)
2. **Single-strand binding proteins** stabilise separated strands
3. **DNA polymerase** adds complementary nucleotides in 5'→3' direction
4. **Leading strand:** Synthesised continuously
5. **Lagging strand:** Synthesised in fragments (**Okazaki fragments**); joined by **DNA ligase**
6. Each new DNA molecule = one old strand + one new strand

### 6.2 Transcription

1. **RNA polymerase** binds to **promoter** region
2. DNA unwinds; template strand read 3'→5'
3. Complementary RNA synthesised 5'→3'
4. Uracil pairs with Adenine (not Thymine)
5. **Pre-mRNA** processed: introns spliced out, exons joined; 5' cap and poly-A tail added
6. Mature **mRNA** leaves nucleus via nuclear pore

### 6.3 Translation

1. mRNA binds to small ribosomal subunit
2. **tRNA** brings amino acids; anticodon pairs with codon
3. **Start codon:** AUG (methionine)
4. Peptide bond forms between amino acids
5. Ribosome moves along mRNA (**translocation**)
6. **Stop codon** (UAA, UAG, UGA) reached → polypeptide released

### 6.4 The Genetic Code

- **Triplet code:** Three bases = one codon = one amino acid
- **Degenerate:** Most amino acids coded by multiple codons
- **Non-overlapping:** Each base part of only one codon
- **Universal:** Same code in almost all organisms
- **Start codon:** AUG; **Stop codons:** UAA, UAG, UGA

### 6.5 Mutations

**Point mutations**
- **Substitution:** One base replaced → may be silent, missense, or nonsense
- **Insertion/Deletion:** Frameshift mutation → alters all downstream amino acids

**Chromosomal mutations**
- Deletion, duplication, inversion, translocation

**Effects:**
- **Silent:** No change in amino acid
- **Missense:** Different amino acid
- **Nonsense:** Premature stop codon
- **Frameshift:** Altered reading frame (insertions/deletions)

## 7. Transport in Plants

### 7.1 Xylem

- Transports **water and minerals** from roots to leaves
- **Dead cells** at maturity; lignified walls; no end walls (continuous tubes)
- **Lignin:** Waterproofs, strengthens, allows lateral water movement (pits)
- **Cohesion-tension theory:**
  1. Transpiration creates tension (negative pressure) in xylem
  2. Water molecules **cohere** (hydrogen bonds)
  3. Water pulled up in continuous column
  4. **Adhesion** to xylem walls helps counteract gravity

### 7.2 Phloem

- Transports **sucrose and amino acids** (assimilates) by **translocation**
- **Living cells:** sieve tube elements + companion cells
- **Source:** Where assimilates produced (e.g., leaf)
- **Sink:** Where assimilates used/stored (e.g., root, fruit, growing tissue)

**Mass Flow (Pressure Flow) Hypothesis**
1. At source: sucrose loaded into phloem → water follows by osmosis → high hydrostatic pressure
2. At sink: sucrose removed → water follows by osmosis → low hydrostatic pressure
3. Pressure gradient drives sap flow from source to sink

**Loading at source:** Active transport of sucrose into companion cells → diffusion into sieve tube elements (symplastic or apoplastic pathway)

### 7.3 Transpiration

- Loss of water vapour from aerial parts of plant (mainly stomata)
- **Stomata:** Guard cells control opening/closing
  - Turgid guard cells → stoma opens (thin outer walls bulge)
- **Factors increasing transpiration rate:**
  - High temperature
  - High light intensity (stomata open)
  - Low humidity
  - High wind speed
  - High water availability in soil

**Xerophyte adaptations:**
- Sunken stomata, reduced leaf surface (spines), rolled leaves, thick cuticle, hairs, CAM photosynthesis

## 8. Transport in Animals

### 8.1 Blood Components

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Erythrocytes (RBCs)** | Transport O₂ (haemoglobin); no nucleus; biconcave disc shape |
| **Leucocytes (WBCs)** | Defence: phagocytes (engulf pathogens), lymphocytes (produce antibodies) |
| **Platelets** | Blood clotting (release thromboplastin) |
| **Plasma** | Transports nutrients, hormones, CO₂, urea, heat; 90% water |

### 8.2 The Heart

**Double circulatory system:**
- **Pulmonary circuit:** Heart → lungs → heart
- **Systemic circuit:** Heart → body → heart

**Cardiac Cycle**
1. **Atrial systole:** Atria contract → blood pushed into ventricles
2. **Ventricular systole:** Ventricles contract → AV valves close (lub); blood ejected through semilunar valves
3. **Diastole:** All chambers relax; semilunar valves close (dub); blood fills atria

**Valves prevent backflow**
- **AV valves** (tricuspid, bicuspid/mitral) — between atria and ventricles
- **Semilunar valves** — between ventricles and arteries

**Cardiac muscle:** Myogenic (self-exciting)
- **Sinoatrial node (SAN):** Pacemaker; initiates wave of excitation
- **Atrioventricular node (AVN):** Delays impulse to allow ventricular filling
- **Bundle of His** and **Purkyne fibres:** Conduct impulse to ventricles

### 8.3 Blood Vessels

| Vessel | Wall Thickness | Lumen | Valves | Function |
|--------|---------------|-------|--------|----------|
| **Arteries** | Thick muscular/elastic | Small | No | Carry blood away from heart (oxygenated, except pulmonary) |
| **Veins** | Thin | Large | Yes | Carry blood to heart (deoxygenated, except pulmonary) |
| **Capillaries** | One cell thick | Very small | No | Exchange between blood and tissue fluid |

**Tissue fluid:** Plasma minus large proteins; surrounds cells; exchanges nutrients/waste; returns to capillaries (osmotic gradient) or becomes lymph

### 8.4 Haemoglobin and Oxygen Transport

**Haemoglobin structure:**
- Quaternary protein; four polypeptide chains, each with a **haem group** containing Fe²⁺
- Each haem binds one O₂ molecule
- Four O₂ per haemoglobin molecule

**Oxygen Dissociation Curves**
- **Bohr effect:** In tissues (high CO₂, low pH), curve shifts right → haemoglobin releases more O₂
- **Fetal haemoglobin:** Higher affinity for O₂ than adult Hb → shift left
- **Myoglobin:** Very high affinity; stores O₂ in muscle

## 9. Gas Exchange

### 9.1 Features of Efficient Gas Exchange Surfaces

1. **Large surface area**
2. **Thin** (short diffusion distance)
3. **Good blood supply** (maintains concentration gradient)
4. **Moist** (gases dissolve)

### 9.2 Lungs (Humans)

- **Trachea → Bronchi → Bronchioles → Alveoli**
- **Alveoli:** Thin walls (one cell thick), large surface area, surrounded by capillaries, moist lining
- **Surfactant:** Reduces surface tension; prevents alveolar collapse
- **Pleural membrane:** Encloses lungs; pleural fluid reduces friction

**Breathing mechanism:**
- **Inspiration:** External intercostals contract, diaphragm contracts (flattens) → thoracic volume ↑ → pressure ↓ → air drawn in
- **Expiration:** Internal intercostals contract (forced), external/diaphragm relax → volume ↓ → pressure ↑ → air expelled

### 9.3 Gills (Fish)

- **Counter-current exchange:** Water and blood flow in opposite directions
- Maintains concentration gradient along entire gill length
- Highly efficient O₂ extraction

### 9.4 Insects

- **Tracheal system:** Trachea → tracheoles → directly to cells
- **Spiracles:** Openings controlled by valves
- No transport system needed; O₂ delivered directly
- Small size limits diffusion distance

### 9.5 Plants

- **Stomata:** Main site of gas exchange
- **Lenticels:** Woody stems
- Guard cells control opening via K⁺ pump (turgidity)
- **Mesophyll cells:** Spongy mesophyll has air spaces

## 10. Infectious Disease

### 10.1 Types of Pathogens

| Pathogen | Examples | Disease |
|----------|----------|---------|
| **Bacteria** | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *Salmonella* | TB, food poisoning, cholera |
| **Viruses** | HIV, Influenza, Measles | AIDS, flu, measles |
| **Fungi** | *Candida*, *Aspergillus* | Thrush, aspergillosis |
| **Protozoa** | *Plasmodium*, *Trypanosoma* | Malaria, sleeping sickness |
| **Helminths** | *Schistosoma*, tapeworms | Schistosomiasis, taeniasis |

### 10.2 Immune System

**Primary defences:**
- Skin (barrier, antimicrobial secretions)
- Mucous membranes, mucus, cilia
- Stomach acid

**Secondary defences (non-specific):**
- **Phagocytes** (neutrophils, macrophages): Engulf and digest pathogens
  1. Pathogen recognised by surface antigens
  2. Phagocyte engulfs → phagosome formed
  3. Lysosome fuses → digests pathogen
  4. Antigens presented on surface → antigen presentation
- **Natural killer cells:** Destroy infected body cells
- **Inflammation:** Increased blood flow, permeability; attracts phagocytes
- **Interferons:** Proteins that inhibit viral replication

**Tertiary defences (specific immunity):**
- **Lymphocytes:** B cells and T cells
- **Clonal selection:** Lymphocyte with matching receptor proliferates

**B cells (humoral immunity):**
- Differentiate into **plasma cells** → secrete **antibodies**
- Some become **memory B cells**
- Antibodies: specific shape complementary to antigen; agglutinate, neutralise, opsonise pathogens

**T cells (cell-mediated immunity):**
- **Helper T cells:** Release cytokines; activate B cells and cytotoxic T cells
- **Cytotoxic T cells:** Kill infected host cells (release perforin)
- **Memory T cells:** Long-lived; rapid response on re-exposure

**Primary vs Secondary Response**
- Primary: Slow, low antibody levels, IgM first then IgG
- Secondary: Faster, stronger, longer; memory cells activated; mainly IgG

### 10.3 Antibiotics

- Kill or inhibit **bacteria** only (not viruses)
- **Bactericidal:** Kill (e.g., penicillin)
- **Bacteriostatic:** Inhibit growth/reproduction
- **Resistance:** Bacteria with resistance genes survive and reproduce → population becomes resistant
- MRSA (meticillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus*)

### 10.4 HIV/AIDS

- **HIV:** Retrovirus; attacks helper T cells
- RNA genome → reverse transcriptase → DNA → integrates into host genome
- Destroys immune system → **AIDS** (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome)
- Transmission: blood, semen, vaginal fluids, breast milk
- Prevention: safe sex, clean needles, screening blood, antiretroviral therapy (ART)

### 10.5 TB and Malaria

**Tuberculosis (TB)**
- *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*; attacks lungs
- Spread by airborne droplets
- Latent infection possible
- Treatment: long course of antibiotics (e.g., rifampicin, isoniazid)

**Malaria**
- *Plasmodium* protozoa (e.g., *P. falciparum*, *P. vivax*)
- Vector: **Anopheles** mosquito
- Life cycle: mosquito → human liver → RBCs → mosquito
- Symptoms: fever, chills, anaemia
- Prevention: insecticide-treated nets, antimalarial drugs (prophylaxis), vector control

## 11. Biodiversity

### 11.1 Species and Classification

- **Biodiversity:** Variety of living organisms; genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity
- **Species:** Group of similar organisms capable of interbreeding to produce fertile offspring
- **Binomial nomenclature:** Genus + species (italicised, Latin)

**Five Kingdoms (traditional)**
1. Prokaryotae (Monera)
2. Protoctista
3. Fungi
4. Plantae
5. Animalia

**Three-domain system (modern)**
- Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya

### 11.2 Conservation

- **In situ:** In natural habitat (national parks, nature reserves, SSSIs)
- **Ex situ:** Outside natural habitat (zoos, seed banks, botanic gardens)
- **CITES:** International agreement to prevent trade in endangered species

### 11.3 Biodiversity Indices

**Species richness:** Number of different species
**Species evenness:** Relative abundance of each species

**Simpson's Diversity Index (D)**
$$D = 1 - \left(\sum \left(\frac{n}{N}\right)^2\right)$$

Where:
- n = number of individuals of each species
- N = total number of individuals
- **Higher D = higher diversity**

## 12. Photosynthesis

### 12.1 Overview

$$6CO_2 + 6H_2O \xrightarrow{\text{light energy}} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2$$

- Occurs in **chloroplasts**
- Two stages: **light-dependent reactions** and **light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle)**

### 12.2 Light-Dependent Reactions

**Location:** Thylakoid membranes

1. **Photoionisation** of chlorophyll in **photosystem II (PSII)**
   - Light excites electron → electron emitted
   - Electron replaced from photolysis of water: **2H₂O → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ + O₂**

2. Electron passes along **electron transport chain (ETC)**
   - Energy released pumps protons (H⁺) into thylakoid space

3. **Chemiosmosis:** Protons flow back through **ATP synthase** → **ATP** generated

4. Electron reaches **photosystem I (PSI)** → re-excited by light → reduces **NADP⁺** to **NADPH**

**Products:** ATP, NADPH, O₂ (waste)

### 12.3 Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent)

**Location:** Stroma

1. **Carbon fixation:** CO₂ + RuBP (5C) → unstable 6C compound → **2 × PGA (3C)**
   - Enzyme: **RuBisCO**

2. **Reduction:** PGA → GP → GALP (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate)
   - Requires ATP and NADPH from light-dependent reactions

3. **Regeneration:** Most GALP used to regenerate RuBP (requires ATP)
   - Some GALP forms glucose, starch, amino acids, lipids

**Net:** 6 CO₂ → 1 glucose (requires 18 ATP, 12 NADPH)

### 12.4 Factors Affecting Photosynthesis

| Factor | Limitation Effect |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Light intensity** | Low light = low rate; photolysis and ETC limited |
| **CO₂ concentration** | Low CO₂ = low rate; Calvin cycle limited |
| **Temperature** | Affects enzyme rate (RuBisCO); optimum ~25-30°C; high temps denature |

**Law of Limiting Factors:** Rate determined by factor in shortest supply.

## 13. Respiration

### 13.1 Overview

- Release of energy from organic compounds
- **Aerobic:** With O₂; more ATP
- **Anaerobic:** Without O₂; less ATP

### 13.2 Glycolysis

**Location:** Cytoplasm

1. Glucose (6C) → phosphorylated → **hexose bisphosphate** (uses 2 ATP)
2. Split into two **triose phosphate (TP)** molecules
3. TP oxidised → **pyruvate (3C)**
4. Net gain: **2 ATP, 2 NADH** per glucose

### 13.3 Link Reaction

**Location:** Mitochondrial matrix

- Pyruvate → **acetyl CoA** + CO₂
- NAD⁺ reduced to NADH
- Acetyl CoA enters Krebs cycle

### 13.4 Krebs Cycle (Citric Acid Cycle)

**Location:** Mitochondrial matrix

1. Acetyl CoA (2C) + oxaloacetate (4C) → **citrate (6C)**
2. Series of oxidations and decarboxylations
3. Products per glucose: **4 CO₂, 6 NADH, 2 FADH₂, 2 ATP**
4. Oxaloacetate regenerated

### 13.5 Electron Transport Chain (ETC) and Oxidative Phosphorylation

**Location:** Inner mitochondrial membrane (cristae)

1. NADH and FADH₂ donate electrons to ETC
2. Electrons pass through protein complexes → energy released
3. Protons (H⁺) pumped into intermembrane space
4. **Chemiosmosis:** Protons flow through ATP synthase → **ATP**
5. Electrons + protons + O₂ → **H₂O**

**Total ATP yield (approximate):**
- Glycolysis: 2 ATP
- Krebs: 2 ATP
- Oxidative phosphorylation: ~28-34 ATP
- **Total: ~30-38 ATP per glucose**

### 13.6 Anaerobic Respiration

**In animals (lactic acid fermentation):**
- Pyruvate → **Lactate** (reduced by NADH)
- Regenerates NAD⁺ for glycolysis to continue
- **Oxygen debt:** Lactate removed by conversion back to pyruvate/glycogen (requires O₂)

**In yeast (alcoholic fermentation):**
- Pyruvate → **ethanol + CO₂**
- NAD⁺ regenerated

## 14. Energy & Ecosystems

### 14.1 Food Chains and Webs

- **Producer:** Autotroph (photosynthetic organism)
- **Primary consumer:** Herbivore
- **Secondary consumer:** Carnivore/omnivore
- **Tertiary consumer:** Top carnivore
- **Decomposers:** Break down dead organic matter

### 14.2 Pyramids

| Pyramid | Shape | Units |
|---------|-------|-------|
| **Number** | May be inverted | Individuals |
| **Biomass** | Usually pyramid | Mass per unit area |
| **Energy** | Always pyramid | Energy per unit area per unit time |

**Why energy pyramid is always upright:**
- Only ~10% energy transferred between trophic levels
- Rest lost as heat (respiration), inedible parts, undigested material, excretion

### 14.3 Energy Transfer

$$\text{Gross Primary Production (GPP)} = \text{Net Primary Production (NPP)} + \text{Respiration (R)}$$

$$NPP = GPP - R$$

- **NPP:** Energy available to primary consumers
- **Efficiency of energy transfer:** Typically 5-20%

### 14.4 Nutrient Cycles

**Carbon Cycle**
1. CO₂ removed by **photosynthesis**
2. CO₂ returned by **respiration**, **combustion**, **decomposition**
3. **Fossilisation:** Carbon stored as coal/oil/limestone
4. Ocean absorbs CO₂ (dissolved, carbonates)

**Nitrogen Cycle**
| Process | Organism/Enzyme | Description |
|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| **Nitrogen fixation** | Nitrogen-fixing bacteria (e.g., *Rhizobium* in root nodules) | N₂ → ammonia/NH₄⁺ |
| **Nitrification** | Nitrifying bacteria (e.g., *Nitrosomonas*, *Nitrobacter*) | NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ |
| **Ammonification** | Decomposers (saprobionts) | Organic N → NH₄⁺ |
| **Denitrification** | Denitrifying bacteria (anaerobic) | NO₃⁻ → N₂ |
| **Assimilation** | Plants | NO₃⁻ / NH₄⁺ → amino acids/proteins |

## 15. Homeostasis

### 15.1 Principles

- **Homeostasis:** Maintenance of internal environment despite external changes
- **Negative feedback:** Counteracts change → restores set point
- **Positive feedback:** Amplifies change (rare; e.g., blood clotting, childbirth)
- Receptor → Control centre → Effector

### 15.2 Temperature Regulation

**Ectotherms:** Body temperature varies with environment
- Behavioural adaptations: basking, shade-seeking, nocturnal activity

**Endotherms:** Maintain constant body temperature (~37°C)

| Response to Cold | Response to Heat |
|------------------|------------------|
| Vasoconstriction (reduces blood flow to skin) | Vasodilation (increases blood flow to skin) |
| Shivering (muscle contraction generates heat) | Sweating (evaporative cooling) |
| Increased metabolic rate (thyroxine/adrenaline) | Reduced metabolic rate |
| Erector pili muscles contract (hair stands up) | Erector pili relax |
| Behavioural: curl up, huddle | Behavioural: seek shade, pant |

**Hypothalamus:** Thermoregulatory centre; contains temperature receptors; receives input from skin thermoreceptors

### 15.3 Blood Glucose Regulation

**Normal range:** ~4-6 mmol/L (fasting)

**After meal (high glucose):**
1. β-cells in **pancreatic islets (Islets of Langerhans)** detect high glucose
2. **Insulin** secreted into blood
3. Insulin binds to receptors on liver/muscle cells
4. Effects:
   - Increased glucose uptake from blood
   - Increased glycogenesis (glucose → glycogen)
   - Increased glycolysis and respiration
   - Decreased gluconeogenesis
5. Blood glucose falls

**During fasting/exercise (low glucose):**
1. **α-cells** detect low glucose
2. **Glucagon** secreted
3. Effects on liver:
   - Glycogenolysis (glycogen → glucose)
   - Gluconeogenesis (amino acids/fats → glucose)
   - Decreased glycolysis
4. Blood glucose rises

**Diabetes Mellitus**
- **Type 1:** Autoimmune destruction of β-cells; no insulin production; insulin injections
- **Type 2:** Insulin resistance; β-cells still function; managed by diet, exercise, medication

### 15.4 Osmoregulation

**Osmoregulation:** Regulation of water and solute concentrations

**Nephron function:**
1. **Ultrafiltration:** High pressure in glomerulus forces water, ions, glucose, urea into Bowman's capsule (filtrate); proteins and cells retained
2. **Selective reabsorption:**
   - **Proximal convoluted tubule:** All glucose, most water and salts reabsorbed (active transport + osmosis)
   - **Loop of Henle:** Establishes medullary gradient (counter-current multiplier); concentrates urine
   - **Distal convoluted tubule:** Fine-tuning of ion balance
3. **Collecting duct:** Water reabsorption controlled by **ADH (antidiuretic hormone/vasopressin)**
   - High blood osmolarity → posterior pituitary releases ADH → aquaporins inserted in collecting duct membrane → more water reabsorbed → concentrated urine
   - Low osmolarity → less ADH → dilute urine

## 16. Coordination

### 16.1 Nervous System

**Divisions:**
- **Central Nervous System (CNS):** Brain and spinal cord
- **Peripheral Nervous System (PNS):** Sensory and motor neurones
  - **Somatic:** Voluntary control of skeletal muscle
  - **Autonomic:** Involuntary
    - **Sympathetic:** "Fight or flight"
    - **Parasympathetic:** "Rest and digest"

**Neurone structure:**
- **Cell body:** Nucleus and organelles
- **Dendrons/dendrites:** Receive impulses
- **Axon:** Conducts impulses away
- **Myelin sheath:** Insulating layer (Schwann cells); speeds conduction (saltatory conduction)
- **Nodes of Ranvier:** Gaps in myelin
- **Motor end plate:** Synapse with effector

**Sensory, relay, and motor neurones**

### 16.2 Action Potentials

**Resting potential:** ~-70 mV (inside negative)
- Maintained by **Na⁺/K⁺ pump** (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in)

**Depolarisation:**
1. Stimulus → voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open
2. Na⁺ rushes in → membrane potential rises to **+40 mV**

**Repolarisation:**
3. Na⁺ channels close; K⁺ channels open
4. K⁺ leaves cell → potential falls

**Hyperpolarisation:**
5. Brief overshoot below resting potential

**Return to resting:** Na⁺/K⁺ pump restores ion balance

**All-or-nothing principle:** Threshold stimulus required; amplitude constant

### 16.3 Synapses

**Structure:** Presynaptic neurone, synaptic cleft, postsynaptic neurone

1. Action potential reaches presynaptic membrane
2. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → Ca²⁺ enters
3. Synaptic vesicles fuse with membrane → **neurotransmitter** released (e.g., acetylcholine)
4. Neurotransmitter diffuses across cleft → binds to receptors on postsynaptic membrane
5. Ligand-gated Na⁺ channels open → depolarisation (excitatory) or hyperpolarisation (inhibitory)
6. Neurotransmitter removed by enzyme breakdown or reuptake

**Properties:**
- **Unidirectional**
- **Summation:** Temporal and spatial
- **Inhibition:** Some synapses hyperpolarise
- **Plasticity:** Basis of learning and memory

### 16.4 Receptors

- **Pacinian corpuscle:** Mechanoreceptor; pressure/vibration
  - Lamellae deform → Na⁺ channels open → generator potential
- **Rods and cones:** Photoreceptors in retina
  - Rods: Low light, black/white
  - Cones: Colour, bright light (red, green, blue)
- **Chemoreceptors:** Taste, smell, CO₂/O₂ levels in blood

### 16.5 Hormones

- Chemical messengers in blood; produced by endocrine glands
- Slower but longer-lasting than nerve impulses
- Target cells have specific receptors

| Gland | Hormone | Target/Effect |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| **Pituitary** | TSH, ACTH, FSH, LH, GH, ADH, oxytocin | Master gland; controls other glands |
| **Thyroid** | Thyroxine (T₄)** | Metabolic rate, growth, development |
| **Adrenal medulla** | Adrenaline | Fight or flight |
| **Adrenal cortex** | Cortisol | Stress response, metabolism |
| **Pancreas** | Insulin, glucagon | Blood glucose |
| **Ovary** | Oestrogen, progesterone | Menstrual cycle, pregnancy |
| **Testis** | Testosterone | Sperm production, secondary sexual characteristics |

### 16.6 Plant Responses

**Phototropism:** Growth in response to light
- **Auxin (IAA):** Plant hormone
- Light causes auxin to accumulate on shaded side
- More cell elongation on shaded side → shoot bends toward light

**Geotropism (Gravitropism):** Response to gravity
- Roots: Positive geotropism (auxin inhibits elongation on lower side)
- Shoots: Negative geotropism

**Gibberellins:** Stem elongation, seed germination, flowering
**Abscisic acid (ABA):** Stomatal closure, dormancy, inhibits growth

## 17. Selection & Evolution

### 17.1 Natural Selection

1. **Variation** exists within populations (mutations, recombination)
2. **Struggle for existence:** Competition for limited resources
3. **Differential survival:** Individuals with advantageous alleles more likely to survive
4. **Reproduction:** Advantageous alleles passed to offspring
5. Over generations: allele frequencies change → evolution

**Evolution:** Change in allele frequencies in a population over time

### 17.2 Speciation

- Formation of new species
- Requires **reproductive isolation**

**Allopatric speciation:**
1. Population separated by **geographic barrier**
2. Different selection pressures
3. Genetic drift, mutations accumulate
4. Reproductive isolation evolves
5. Cannot interbreed even if barrier removed

**Sympatric speciation:**
- Occurs without geographic separation (e.g., polyploidy in plants)

### 17.3 Artificial Selection

- Humans select desirable traits
- Examples: Crop plants, domestic animals
- Reduces genetic diversity

### 17.4 Antibiotic Resistance

1. Random mutation creates resistance gene
2. Antibiotic acts as selective agent
3. Resistant bacteria survive and reproduce
4. Resistance spreads (vertical and horizontal gene transfer)
5. MRSA and other superbugs emerge

**Reducing resistance:**
- Complete antibiotic courses
- Restrict agricultural use
- Develop new antibiotics
- Use combination therapies

## 18. Genetic Technology

### 18.1 Recombinant DNA Technology

1. **Isolation:** Gene of interest identified and extracted (using **restriction enzymes**)
2. **Cutting:** Restriction enzymes cut DNA at specific recognition sites (palindromic)
3. **Ligation:** Gene inserted into **plasmid vector** using **DNA ligase**
4. **Transformation:** Plasmid introduced into host bacteria (e.g., heat shock, electroporation)
5. **Selection:** Marker genes (e.g., antibiotic resistance) identify transformed cells
6. **Expression:** Host produces protein

### 18.2 PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction)

Amplifies DNA in vitro — millions of copies from tiny sample

**Steps (repeated 20-40 cycles):**
1. **Denaturation:** 95°C → DNA strands separate
2. **Annealing:** 50-65°C → Primers bind to complementary sequences
3. **Extension:** 72°C → Taq polymerase extends primers

**Requirements:** Template DNA, primers, nucleotides, Taq polymerase (thermostable), buffer, Mg²⁺

### 18.3 Gel Electrophoresis

- Separates DNA fragments by size
- DNA loaded into wells in agarose gel
- Electric current applied — DNA moves toward positive electrode (negative charge on phosphate)
- **Smaller fragments move faster/further**
- Stained with ethidium bromide or fluorescent dye

### 18.4 Genetic Engineering Applications

| Application | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| **Medicine** | Human insulin from bacteria, growth hormone, clotting factors |
| **Agriculture** | Bt cotton (insect resistance), Golden Rice (vitamin A) |
| **Research** | Gene knockout studies, model organisms |
| **Gene therapy** | Inserting functional gene (e.g., CFTR for cystic fibrosis) |

### 18.5 Cloning

**Reproductive cloning (Dolly the sheep):**
1. Nucleus removed from egg cell (enucleation)
2. Nucleus from somatic (body) cell inserted
3. Electric shock stimulates division
4. Embryo implanted into surrogate mother

**Therapeutic cloning:**
- Clone used to produce embryonic stem cells for research/treatment

**Stem cells:**
- **Totipotent:** Can form any cell + placenta (zygote)
- **Pluripotent:** Can form any cell type (embryonic stem cells)
- **Multipotent:** Can form limited cell types (adult stem cells)

**Ethical considerations:**
- GM crops: Environmental impact, gene flow, corporate control
- Human cloning: Safety, identity, exploitation
- Gene therapy: Germline therapy concerns, accessibility

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Confusing **mitosis** (2 identical diploid cells) and **meiosis** (4 different haploid cells)
- Forgetting **O₂ is a waste product** of photosynthesis (from photolysis)
- Confusing **DNA polymerase** (replication) and **RNA polymerase** (transcription)
- Mixing up **competitive** (active site) and **non-competitive** (allosteric site) inhibition
- Saying enzymes are "killed" — they are **denatured**
- Forgetting **carbon fixation** is catalysed by **RuBisCO**
- Confusing **source** and **sink** in phloem transport
- Mixing up **vasoconstriction** (cold) and **vasodilation** (heat)
- Forgetting **immature xylem** can be living (before lignification)
